# English Idioms

A capture list plus a short classroom PDF (**Morgan**, 7 pp., 377 numbered idioms with one-line meanings). Do not dump the 377 into the wiki; use Morgan to fill Meaning, not as etymology. Dates in the vault table are still mostly blank. Treat source-domain tags as working guesses. ^[ambiguous]

## Key Ideas

Idioms are semi-fixed phrases whose meaning is not the sum of the words — the same phenomenon [[concepts/pattern-grammar]] and [[concepts/kurdi-lexical-semantics]] describe as units of meaning / figurative extension.

**Nautical cluster (tagged “sailing” in the list):** *three sheets to the wind*; *a rising tide lifts all boats*; *by and large*; *missed the boat*; *we’re all in the same boat*; *hook, line and sinker*.

**Horse / animal:** *don’t look a gift horse in the mouth*; *hold your horses*; *straight from the horse’s mouth*; *don’t put the cart before the horse*; *crying wolf*; *let the cat out of the bag*; *kill two birds with one stone*; *a bird in the hand…*; *night owl* / *early bird*.

**Military (tagged):** *short fuse*; *went down in flames*.

**Cards / games:** *not playing with a full deck*; *play your cards right*; *the ball is in your court*; *whole new ballgame*; *down for the count*.

**Weather / body:** *under the weather*; *when it rains, it pours*; *on thin ice*; *cold feet*; *cost an arm and a leg*; *by the skin of his teeth*.

The list also collects insult/intelligence tropes (*not the sharpest tack*, *dumber than a bag of hammers*) and workplace speech (*nip it in the bud*, *elephant in the room*, *burn the midnight oil*). Do not treat the table as etymologies.
