# Topics in Old English research

[[synthesis/Research: Old English Scholarship 2016–2026|Review overview]] · [[references/old-english-2016-2026-literature-map|Full literature map]] · [[concepts/old-english-research-methodologies|Methodology guide]]

Topics answer what a work investigates. The following 15 families organize this retained corpus; overlapping membership is intentional. Batch 2 added names and inscriptions. Batch 3 enlarged both families, and enlarged religion, poetry, and manuscripts, from publisher pages that were not in the first chart. Counts are properties of the map, not estimates of the field. Language, poetry, law, manuscript evidence, and modern reception often intersect.

## Boundaries that matter

Medieval education is distinct from modern teaching research. A text about healing may be studied philologically, cognitively, ethically, or experimentally; medicine is its topic, not a single method. Reception includes modern and early modern uses of Old English, while post-Conquest manuscript transmission can still concern the history of the language itself.

Compare [[sources/oe-brookman-robinson-2016-teaching|Brookman and Robinson on teaching]] and [[sources/oe-sweany-2020-comparison|Sweany on medical comparison]]. For the difference between evidence and interpretation, see [[concepts/old-english-research-methodologies]].
