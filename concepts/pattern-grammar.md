# Pattern Grammar

From Chapelle (ed.), *Concise Encyclopedia of Applied Linguistics*: Hunston/Francis/Sinclair line. Grammar is observed as **recurring sequences** around a lexical item, not a system independent of words.

## Key Ideas

- Firth → Halliday → Sinclair: reject a sharp meaning/form split and a lexis/grammar split. Units of meaning are semi-fixed phrases. **Colligation** is co-occurrence with grammatical features (clause type, specific prepositions).
- Example: *RESOLVE* — NP object “solve a problem”; *to*-infinitive “decide”; *into* “divide.” Three patterns, three senses.
- COBUILD notation: drop the subject NP as redundant; capitals for the target word; italics for literal words. *She transformed him from X into Y* → `V n from n into n`. Dummy-*it* adjectives: `it v-link ADJ for n to-inf`.
- Francis et al. list verbs/nouns/adjectives per pattern, grouped by meaning. *Struggle* with `N with n` (conflict) vs `N to-inf` (attempt) vs `it v-link N to-inf` (difficulty). Prepositions **classify** which facet of a word is highlighted (*BREAK into* physical vs *BREAK with* interpersonal vs *BREAK news to*).
- Pedagogy: consciousness-raising; measuring learner verb+preposition development. Discourse: stance patterns differ by discipline (Charles; Groom).
- Vs **construction grammar**: both pair word, pattern, and meaning. Constructions are claimed to be cognitive; patterns are observational. Hunston & Su: each meaning-group of a pattern might be a construction (`ADJ at n` → “good at” vs “emotion at”).
