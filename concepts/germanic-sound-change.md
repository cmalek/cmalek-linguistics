# Germanic Sound Change and Reconstruction

Named sound laws are **layers**, not rival theories. Reconstruction works because outcomes are regular enough to compare. See [[concepts/historical-linguistics-sound-terms]] for the phonetic vocabulary.

## Key Ideas

- Sequence: reconstructed PIE → shared Germanic innovations ([[concepts/germanic-sound-change|Grimm's Law]], Verner's Law) → Proto-Germanic → later branch changes in [[concepts/old-english]], Old Norse, Old High German → analogy that can hide older phonology.
- **Grimm's Law** is Germanic-only. Compactly: PIE voiceless stops → fricatives, voiced stops → voiceless stops, voiced aspirates → voiced fricatives/stops. Classic comparison: Latin *pater* vs. Gothic *fadar* / OE *fæder*. Greek, Sanskrit, Latin, and Romance do not undergo Grimm; they are comparison points.
- **Verner's Law** regularizes Grimm “exceptions”: some fricatives voiced when the *preceding* syllable did **not** carry the inherited PIE accent.
- **i-mutation** (i-umlaut) is much later, branch-level vowel history: a following *i* or *j* fronts a preceding vowel (*mann ~ menn*, *fōt ~ fēt*). Umlaut is this kind of local trigger; **ablaut** is older inherited vowel alternation in a paradigm. *fōt ~ fēt* can show both.
- **ON breaking** (EIEOL lesson 2): Proto-Norse *e* → *ja*, and *jǫ* when a following *u* is (or was) present — *gjalda* beside OE *geldan*; blocked after *v, l, r*. Mechanism still debated. See [[concepts/old-norse-grammar]].
- West Saxon, Mercian, Northumbrian, and Kentish are **late dialects inside OE**, not Indo-European branches. A change may look regular in WS and differ in timing or spelling in Anglian or Kentish. See [[concepts/old-english-dialects]].
- After Proto-Germanic, **branch-level** processes (i-mutation, a-mutation, o-mutation, breaking, raising) reshape vowels. **Local/prosodic** processes (syncope, assimilation, gemination, reduction) shorten and reshape forms. **Analogy and leveling** then hide older phonology.
- **Breaking:** a vowel becomes a diphthong or more complex vowel near certain consonants — very common in OE spelling histories.
- **Palatalization:** consonants pull toward the palate near front vowels or *j*; beginner reminder: OE `c`/`g` can stay velar or move toward *ch*/*y*; dotted `ċ`/`ġ` is modern teaching notation, not a native manuscript norm.
- Beginner rule of thumb: Grimm/Verner are earlier and broader; mutations like i-umlaut are later and more local.
- Causes of Grimm's first shift are not settled; Lehmann notes no widely accepted physical explanation. Intervocalic *h* loss is ordinary lenition in a weak environment. ^[inferred]
- Order of famous changes is inferred from **dependency** (what must precede what), not from knowing the articulatory cause: Grimm before Verner (Verner voices Grimm’s fricatives); mobile PIE accent still relevant at Verner; fixed Germanic stress and i-mutation later.
