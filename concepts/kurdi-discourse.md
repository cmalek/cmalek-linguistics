# Kurdi Discourse

From Kurdi (2017), ch. 3. Since Harris (1952) many linguists reject Chomsky’s Standard Theory claim that the **sentence** is the maximal unit. *Discourse* is one of the most polysemous terms in the field; the cleanest definitions are negative.

## Key Ideas

- Ancient rhetoric already planned extra-sentential speech (legal/political), but normatively. Modern extra-sentential work is split across functional, cognitive, socio-, textual linguistics and DA — united by rejecting sentence-maximality.
- **Discourse vs speech:** some (Guillaume tradition) treat discourse as *langue* in use, preferring language/discourse to Saussure’s language/speech because *speech* sounds oral-only.
- **Discourse vs sentence:** generally supra-sentential; Benveniste: relations *between* sentences are a different kind from phoneme/morpheme/syntax relations.
- **Discourse vs narrative:** Benveniste — discourse is anchored in the utterance situation (*here/now*, often *I*); narrative detaches, prefers third person and past tenses.
- **Discourse vs text:** several incompatible tables of criteria (not unpacked here).
