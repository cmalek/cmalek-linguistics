# Paired and Group Oral Assessment

Chapelle encyclopedia (Sonca Vo): candidates talk to each other while examiners rate. Grew with communicative teaching; Cambridge Main Suite paired tasks; China’s CET-SET uses groups of three or four.

## Key Ideas

**Why testers like it:** cheaper than 1:1; examiner can rate without also being interlocutor; often less intimidating; closer to classroom pair work; elicits **interactional** strategies (negotiation of meaning, scaffolding) that interviews suppress. ffrench (2003): 26 of 30 target functions in the paired format vs 14 in interviews. Peer talk is more symmetric than examiner-led interviews (van Lier).

**Why it fails:** peers may sequential-monologue, dominate, or skip functions. He & Dai on CET-SET: fewer than one third produced challenging/supporting/persuading; almost no meaning-negotiation unless the task is framed with a pragmatic purpose.

**Fairness:** interlocutor variables (gender, proficiency mismatch, personality, L1, acquaintanceship) show **mixed, context-dependent** effects. O'Sullivan: Japanese vs Turkish pairings reversed. Testers treat interlocutor effects as construct-irrelevant variance: task design, planned pairing, extra tasks in high-stakes batteries.

Online avatar group tests exist (Ockey, Gu, Keehner).
