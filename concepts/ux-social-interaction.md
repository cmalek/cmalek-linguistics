# Social interaction and collaborative interfaces

Chapter 5 treats communication as a coordinated activity. Greetings, turn-taking, and the organization of a conversation matter when designing communication tools and dialogues with digital agents.

Remote communication and co-presence pose different design problems. Remote systems mediate the cues people use to coordinate. Co-located systems, such as shared displays, must support people acting together in the same space. Awareness of other people's actions and progress helps coordination.

| Setting | Design question |
| --- | --- |
| Remote conversation | Which conversational cues survive the medium? |
| Shared physical space | How do people coordinate concurrent actions? |
| Social engagement | What supports voluntary participation and exchange? |

[[concepts/ux-interface-selection]] supplies the available media, while [[concepts/ux-cognition]] explains why information can be distributed across people and artifacts.
