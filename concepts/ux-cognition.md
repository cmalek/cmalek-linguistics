# Cognition in interaction design

Cognition includes attention, perception, memory, learning, language processing, and reflective activities such as planning. Chapter 4 distinguishes skilled, experiential action from deliberate reflection; a design may need to support both.

## Design implications

Attention depends on goals and the salience of relevant information. Grouping and spacing help people locate information. Interruptions can impose a cost when people resume a task. Memory is selective, and learning depends on retaining experience.

| Framework | What it examines |
| --- | --- |
| Mental models | How a person understands and reasons about a system |
| Gulfs of execution and evaluation | Moving from intention to action, then interpreting the result |
| Information processing | Stages of processing information |
| Distributed cognition | People, representations, tools, and their coordination |
| External cognition | How external representations and tools support thought |
| Embodied interaction | Meaning through engagement with the physical and social world |
