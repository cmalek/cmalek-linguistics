# Tolkien and Old English

Working list of legendarium names the notes derive from [[concepts/old-english]]. Rohan is the densest cluster: Tolkien used OE as the “translated” language of the Mark.

## Key Ideas

**Secure-looking Rohan matches (notes):**

| Name | OE cited |
|---|---|
| Orthanc | *orþanc* “skill” |
| Isengard | *īsen* + *geard* (Gimli’s “iron stronghold”) |
| Eorl | *eorl* |
| éored | *ēored* cavalry/troop |
| Théoden | *þēoden* prince/king |
| Thengel / Fengel | *þengel* / *fengel* prince |
| Gríma | *grīma* mask/visor |
| Gálmód | *gāl-mōd* licentious |
| Edoras | *edoras* enclosed dwellings |
| mearas | *mearh* horse |
| Hornburg | *horn* + *burg* |
| The Mark | not OE; Danish *mark* “country” as in Denmark |

**Other:** *athelas* ~ *æþel* noble, eminent (added in the revised notes); Déagol ~ *dīgol* secret; Ent = *ent* giant; *flet* floor/hall; Beorn *beorn* warrior; mathom *māþum* treasure; Galdor *galdor* incantation.

**Flagged in the notes or here:**

- *Éomund / Éomer / Éowyn*: the revised notes derive *eo-* from *eoh* "horse" + *mund* "protector", *mǣre* "famous" and *wynn* "pride of its kind". This replaces the earlier reading of *eo-* as *wesan* subjunctive "I shall be", which was a stretch. The *Éomer* = horse + famous reading is still called "a bit stretched" in the notes, though he did become great. ^[inferred]
- *Westu Théoden hál*: *westu* is not a normal attested spelling; the greeting is the familiar *wes þū hāl* type. ^[ambiguous]
- Háma as *hāma* “cricket” because he announces visitors is playful, not demonstrated. ^[inferred]
- Helm vs French diminutive *-et* on *helmet* is a modern-English aside.

Tolkien also wrote on translating [[references/beowulf]].

## Maldon

His verse play *The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth Beorhthelm’s Son*, with the essay “Ofermod,” reads Byrhtnoth’s grant of the causeway as overmastering pride. That reading set the terms of later argument about [[references/battle-of-maldon]]. The word, the counter-readings, and the Moria parallel critics have drawn are on [[concepts/ofermod]].

A 2013 survey of Old English after 1066 names Tolkien among later writers who draw on the language. It adds no name etymologies. See [[concepts/old-english-after-1066]].

## Literary echoes in the Middle-earth clipping

The clipped article adds Meduseld as “mead hall,” Saruman as “skilled person,” and Middle-earth as an English counterpart to middangeard. It connects the Rohirric horse-and-rider lament to *The Wanderer* and its ubi sunt motif, and traces many Hobbit dwarf names to the name list in *Vǫluspá*. These are the article’s claims; the underlying Tolkien and Norse passages were not independently collated.

The article glosses Éowyn as “horse-joy,” differing from the earlier note’s wording for wynn. It also renders Isengard as “steel place,” against the existing “iron stronghold” gloss. Preserve these as differences in explanatory wording rather than evidence for a newly established etymology. ^[ambiguous]
