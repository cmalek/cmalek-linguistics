# Old English Pronunciation

A usable first-pass system for reading [[concepts/old-english]], not a claim of one recovered accent. Older grammars compare to German/French rather than strict IPA; converting those cues to IPA is an approximation.

## Key Ideas

**Vowels (short / long):** `æ` [æ] / `ǣ` [æː]; `a` [ɑ] / `ā` [ɑː]; `e` [ɛ] / `ē` [eː]; `i` [ɪ] / `ī` [iː]; `o` [ɔ] or [o] / `ō` [oː]; `u` [ʊ] / `ū` [uː]; `y` [y] / `ȳ` [yː] (front rounded, not [j]). A pasted trapezium (`attachments/ipa-vowel-trapezium.jpg`) places these on a front/back grid including *ʏ ʊ ɒ* and a low *ä* — MnE IPA, not a reconstructed OE vowel space. See [[references/visual-clippings]] and [[concepts/phonetics-and-phonology]].

**Fricative voicing** (high value for reading): `f`, `s`, and `þ`/`ð` are voiceless initially, finally, doubled, and next to voiceless sounds; voiced between voiced sounds. Scribes did **not** reserve þ for [θ] and ð for [ð] — environment beats the letter. UT EIEOL: dictionary headwords may standardize on one letter (often ð) and sort þ/ð together after T.

**Tricky consonants:**

- `c`: [k] before consonants and in back-vowel environments (*bōc*); learner-safe [tʃ] in palatal / front environments (*cild*, *benc*). Narrow IPA for palatal *c* is not settled (Wright more conservative than many teaching texts).
- `g`: [g] initially before back vowels, before consonants, after [ŋ], when doubled; [ɣ] medially between back vowels; **oldenglish.info** also gives palatal [j] before front vowels (*ġeond*, *werig*), which 0003 treats more as [ɣ] in back environments — palatal *g* is a teaching split. ^[ambiguous]
- `n` is [ŋ] before velars; `r` as a light tap [ɾ] is a classroom compromise.
- `sc` is usually [ʃ] (*scip*, *fisc*); [sk] in compounds (*iscald*) and next to back vowels (*ascian*, *tusc*).
- `cg` is the *bridge/edge* affricate (info page writes [ʒ]; treat as [dʒ] in classroom English). ^[ambiguous]
- Manuscript **wynn** (ƿ) = [w]; modern editions often respell *w*. Alphabet extras: þ ð æ; no native *j v w z* letters.
- **No silent letters.**

**Teaching diacritics** (not in the manuscripts): a macron marks a long vowel (`ā ǣ ē ī ō ū ȳ`); a dot on `ċ`/`ġ` marks the palatal teaching values. The marked Passion text is a worked example — see [[references/passion-peter-and-paul]] and [[concepts/phonetics-and-phonology]] (pronunciation vs phonics vs IPA).

See [[concepts/historical-linguistics-sound-terms]] for stop/fricative/palatal vocabulary used in [[concepts/germanic-sound-change]].
