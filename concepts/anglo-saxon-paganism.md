# Anglo-Saxon Paganism

Beliefs and cult of the [[entities/anglo-saxons]] from the mid-fifth to eighth centuries: a regional variant of Germanic paganism, not a single church. *Pagan* / OE *hæðen* are Christian pejoratives; there is no evidence that practitioners used a name for “their religion.” Neil Price: in this context paganism is “largely an empty concept defined by what it is not (Christianity).”

## Key Ideas

- Polytheism centred on *ése* (sg. *ós*). Prominent gods: **Woden** (kings’ genealogies), **Thunor**, **Tiw**. Other landscape beings: elves, nicors, dragons. Weekday names in English preserve the deities.
- Cult: offerings of objects and animals; festivals; timber temples *and* open-air trees/megaliths. Funerary mix of inhumation and cremation with grave goods. Magic/witchcraft; some scholars speak of shamanic elements. Afterlife ideas are poorly known.
- Evidence is poor compared with Ireland, Francia, or Scandinavia: [[entities/bede]], Aldhelm, *Life of St Wilfrid* (Latin, uninterested in a full portrait); Boniface/Willibrord on the continent; Tacitus on *ancestors*; place-names (mostly south/midlands — rare in Northumbria and East Anglia); archaeology (burials, metalwork) mostly from the conversion period.
- Using Norse myth as a guide is tempting and disputed (Stenton: the connection was already remote; Branston: common ancestry justifies it). ^[ambiguous]
- Conversion ([[concepts/christianity-in-anglo-saxon-england]]) is aristocratic and staggered c. 597–680s. Folklore and penitential bans on “demons” show leftover practice; by a generation after Wihtred’s laws (695), Bede writes as if paganism is gone.
- Scholars prefer “multiple paganisms” or “traditional religion”; “pre-Christian” is often chronologically inexact because overlap lasted decades.

[[references/beowulf]] sits in this problem: a Christian manuscript telling a pagan-Scandinavian story.
