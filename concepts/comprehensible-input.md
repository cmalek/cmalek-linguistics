# Comprehensible Input

Lonsdale: “When you first understand the message, you will acquire the language unconsciously.” The Medium article names this as **Krashen’s** result: learners using comprehensible input outperform those drilling formal grammar.

## Key Ideas

- Input must be understandable from context, gesture, and known language — not a wall of adult talk over a beginner’s head (the drowning/immersion myth on [[skills/six-month-language-learning]]).
- “Brain soaking” (lots of listening for rhythm before words) and “get meaning before words” are the practical habits attached to this claim.
- The article does not unpack *i+1*, the affective filter, or Monitor Theory; those are standard Krashen labels filled in here. ^[inferred]

For NLP, [[concepts/nlp-corpus]] collections of learner- or child-directed speech (CHILDES) are a research analogue of graded input. ^[inferred]
