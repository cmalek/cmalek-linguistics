# Germanic Language Family

Classroom three-way split. Slocum/Lehmann (UT) is the better source; a PoliLingua marketing post adds a modern-language list and some shaky chronology. ^[ambiguous]

## Key Ideas

**Branches**

| Branch | Early witnesses | Living outcomes (rough) |
|---|---|---|
| West | Old English, Old Saxon, Old Frisian, Old Franconian, Old High German | English, German, Dutch, Afrikaans, Yiddish, Frisian |
| North | Old Norse | Danish, Swedish, Norwegian (Bokmål/Nynorsk), Icelandic, Faroese |
| East | Gothic (best attested); Vandalic, Burgundian poorly attested | none |

Gothic is the earliest *attested* Germanic language (Biblical translation), not the ancestor of English.

**English line:** Anglo-Saxon settlement (5th–6th c.) → OE → Norman French overlay; core grammar/everyday lexicon still West Germanic. Viking-age Old Norse is the North Germanic contact layer ([[concepts/danelaw]]).

**Other West landmarks (PoliLingua, use with care):** Old High German 6th–11th c. and Latin loans; Luther’s 1534 Bible (Saxon dialect) as a written-German standard; later *Hochsprache*. Printing-press standardization is a late-medieval German story, not an OE fact.

**Do not take from PoliLingua:** Proto-Indo-European spoken “around 3,000 years ago” (the wiki’s [[concepts/history-of-english]] uses c. 4500–2500 BCE). Speaker-count tables are advertising. Crimean Gothic “until the 18th century” is a contested leftover-Gothic story (Busbecq’s 16th-c. wordlist is the usual cite). ^[ambiguous]

**Against a too-clean tree (Krause/Slocum ON intro):** ON shares *jj/ww* sharpening and 2sg past *-t* with Gothic; shares umlaut, *-dōm/-skapi*, and dying reduplication with West Germanic. Treat East/North/West as a teaching grid, not isolated lineages. Course: [[skills/ut-eieol-old-norse]].

See [[entities/germanic-peoples]] for tribes and settlement; [[skills/ut-eieol-old-english]] for the same family tree aimed at readers of OE texts.
