# Old English for-, fore-, and oþ- Prefixes

Distilled from a chat-style research note (the claims cite Alcorn, Minkova, Ruiz Narbona and dictionary entries; they were not independently checked here). Extends the prefix list in [[concepts/oe-word-formation]].

## Key Ideas

**for- vs fore- are different prefixes.** *for-* modifies a verb with a privative, pejorative, resultative or intensive sense ("away", "wrongly", "completely", "to destruction"): *forseōn* "despise", *fordōn* "destroy". *fore-* means "before, in front of, in advance": *foreseōn* "foresee", *foresendan* "send ahead". The complication is graphic: unstressed *fore-* can be reduced or written *for-*, so spelling alone may not identify the prefix. [[entities/bosworth-toller]] notes the two are frequently confused despite very different meanings. Verdict: not semantically equivalent, sometimes graphically indistinguishable. ^[inferred]

**The macron in fōre- is editorial.** Manuscripts do not mark vowel length; the macron (or Bosworth–Toller's acute, *fóre-*) is a modern reconstruction. B–T split *for-/fore-* "for, on account of" from *fōr-/fōre-* "before, in front of". Rhona Alcorn found no evidence for a long vowel in any form of FOR and treats *for* and *fore* mainly as prosodically conditioned variants, explicitly criticizing B–T's four-way division. Practical rule: use *fore-* in normalized text unless reproducing a dictionary's conventions; vowel length is not a secure way to separate destructive *for-* from anterior *fore-*. See also [[concepts/old-english-orthographic-variation]].

**oþ- vs ōþ- show no dependable semantic opposition.**

| Editorial form | Proposed origin | Older meaning claim |
|---|---|---|
| *ot-/oþ-* | weak form related to *æt-* | "at, toward, to" (OED-derived) |
| *ōþ-* | separate reconstructed prefix | "from, away" (B–T *óþ-*) |

[[entities/donka-minkova]] (Prefixation and Stress in Old English) treats *ūþ-/oþ-* as strong/weak allomorphs that may be etymologically defensible but were not a living formal contrast in OE. Ruiz Narbona (2018) uses unmarked *oþ-* for the whole range: chiefly "from, away" (*oþwendan* "turn away", *oþwindan* "escape", *oþfeallan* "fall away") but also "at, close to" (*oðstandan* "stand still"). Recommendation: treat *oþ-* as the normalized headword, record "away/from" and "at/to/close" as overlapping senses, and classify compounds by attested meaning rather than vowel length. ^[inferred]
