# Old English c-Palatalization

C can represent velar /k/ or a palatal outcome conventionally read as /tʃ/. The modern dot is an editorial interpretation, not a normal manuscript distinction.

Initial c before an inherited front vowel, medial c before earlier *i/*j, and final c after i/ī are central environments. Later vowel fronting, loss of a trigger, and paradigm levelling obscure the relationship to spelling. Do not treat every present e, i, æ or y as equivalent.

The full rule qualifications, exclusion tables, reverse tables, and complete bounded Wright inventories are in [[synthesis/Research: Old English C-Palatalization]]. A word may contain both categories: cyċene and cryċċ. A form-specific record is safer than a lemma-wide blacklist. ^[inferred]

Palatalization and subsequent assibilation are distinct. For early and northern varieties, a palatal category need not entail [tʃ]. See [[sources/laker-2007-palatalization]] and [[sources/versloot-2023-yorkshire]]. Compare [[concepts/old-english-g-palatalization]] and [[concepts/old-english-pronunciation]].

The principal lexical inventory is [[sources/wright-1914-c-palatalization]], cross-checked with [[sources/freeborn-old-english-pronunciation]].
