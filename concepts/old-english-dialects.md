# Old English Dialects

[[concepts/old-english]] dialects are a late internal layer, not PIE branches. Wright takes early West Saxon as the grammar standard; Northumbrian, Mercian, and Kentish show clear peculiarities in the oldest records. Slocum/Lehmann teach a **three-way** map (West Saxon / Kentish / Anglian); Anglian here is the cover term for Mercian+Northumbrian. See also [[concepts/germanic-sound-change]] and [[skills/ut-eieol-old-english]].

## Key Ideas

| Dialect | Region | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| West Saxon | South of the Thames except Kent and Surrey | Baseline because so much surviving prose is WS. |
| Mercian | Humber to Thames | Anglian, with Northumbrian. |
| Northumbrian | Forth to Humber | Anglian; often non-WS vowels. Survives as Scots. |
| Kentish | Kent and Surrey | Fronted/unrounded spellings. |

- Copied texts **mix** dialects. Spotting is probability from **clusters** of cues, not one letter.
- Strategy: assume WS → look for repeated non-WS vowels (`ea` vs `æ/e`, `y/ie` vs `e`) → Anglian vs Kentish first → only then try Mercian vs Northumbrian.

**High-value cues (Wright):**

- WS `ea` ~ Anglian `æ`/`e`: *ceaster/cæster*, *geat/gæt*, *sceal/scæl*, *meaht/mæht*, *weax/wæx*.
- Kentish often `e` where WS has `æ`/`ea`: *dæg/deg*, *fæder/feder*, *wæter/weter*, *ceaster/cester*.
- Non-WS often simpler `e` vs WS `ie`/`y`: *dierne/derne*, *giest/gest*.
- Kentish *wiodu* vs WS/Anglian *wudu*.
- Mercian umlaut extras: *featu* vs *fatu*, *heafuc* vs *hafoc*, *fearan* vs *faran*.
- Northumbrian: *eorm* vs WS *earm*, *heord* vs *heard*, *æhher* vs *ēar*, *tæhher* vs *tēar*.

Modern English derives mostly from Anglian; classrooms still teach West Saxon because [[entities/alfred-the-great]]'s Wessex preserved the bulk of the manuscript tradition, including much of [[references/beowulf]].

## Open Questions

- Separating Mercian from Northumbrian in mixed copies remains the hard step.
