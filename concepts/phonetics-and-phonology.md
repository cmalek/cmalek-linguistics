# Phonetics and Phonology

From the same Chapelle encyclopedia clipping as [[concepts/pattern-grammar]]. Aligns with Kurdi’s phonetics/phonology split already noted on [[concepts/historical-linguistics-sound-terms]].

## Key Ideas

**Four labels:** *Phonetics* — scientific study of speech (articulatory, acoustic, auditory) as a human capacity, not one language. *Phonology* — how sounds pattern in a **particular** language (Thai [l] only initial; English */tl/ */dl/ gaps). *Pronunciation* — teaching the sounds of a new language. *Phonics* — sound–spelling for literacy.

**Deep history (sketched):** Pāṇini on Sanskrit; Al-Khalil’s Arabic dictionary ordered from larynx to lips.

**19th–20th c. teaching line:** Baudouin de Courtenay (phoneme); Henry Sweet (spoken English notation, also an OE/ON scholar). Daniel Jones: the applied phoneme and **cardinal vowels**; UCL succession Gimson → Wells; Abercrombie → Ladefoged. Firth: prosodic analysis / coarticulation. Instrumental: palatography, electroaerometer, spectrograms (now often Praat), synthesis.

**IPA:** Passy and Jespersen’s teachers’ association (1897). Principles (1888): one sign–one sound; same sign across languages; prefer roman letters; avoid diacritics when possible. Major revision Kiel 1989. Distinct from Gimson/EPD14 **phonemic** English dictionary transcription and from APA (more diacritics, typewriter fieldwork). Machine alphabets: ARPABET, SAMPA, Kirshenbaum.

**Campbell chart (MIT Press, 2nd ed., pp. xxi–xxii):** place × manner grid (bilabial → glottal) for stops, affricates, fricatives, nasals, approximants, laterals; vowels by height/backness with tense/lax pairs (*i/ɪ*, *e/ɛ*, *u/ʊ*). Diacritic page: aspiration *Cʰ*, dental *C̪*, glottalized, labialized, palatalized, voiceless *̥*, length *ː*, nasal *̃*. Note: Campbell often writes *a* for both [a] and [ɑ]. Two vault PDFs (*Phonetic Symbols Chart.pdf* and *… Chart 1.pdf*) are the same three pages. A separate pasted trapezium (`ipa-vowel-trapezium.jpg`) is a simpler front/back teaching chart. Classroom [[concepts/old-english-pronunciation]] macrons and dotted `ċ`/`ġ` remain teaching diacritics, not this chart. ^[inferred]

**SIL:** missionary linguistics; Pike; *Ethnologue* (16th ed. cited: 6,909 languages). Purpose is Bible translation — applied phoneme-based orthographies. Charis SIL, Doulos SIL, Gentium, and Andika are also the fonts used to train IPA OCR in [[references/ocr-for-the-ipa]]. Copying those symbols out of a PDF is [[skills/linguistic-pdf-to-markdown]].

**Phonology after Jones:** Chomsky & Halle *SPE* — segments as bundles of binary features, ordered rules from underlying to surface. Later: natural phonology, autosegmental, OT (constraint ranking vs SPE’s production line). Applied classroom work usually stops at Jones/Gimson taxonomic phonemes (Abercrombie on Jones).
