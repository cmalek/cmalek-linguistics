# Christianity in Anglo-Saxon England

Old English *Crīstendōm*. In the seventh century pagan [[entities/anglo-saxons]] were converted mainly by Roman missionaries; Irish monks from Iona shaped [[concepts/anglo-saxon-kingdoms|Northumbria]]. After the Synod of Whitby (664) the English church aligned with the papacy on Easter and related custom.

## Key Ideas

- **Roman Britain** already had bishops at Arles (314: York, London, possibly Lincoln). How deep Christianity ran in the countryside is disputed (Morris vs Yorke). It survives in western/northern Brittonic kingdoms and, via Patrick and others, in Ireland; “Celtic” vs Roman clashes later centre on Easter date, baptismal custom, and tonsure.
- **Gregorian mission (597):** Augustine lands in Thanet; Æthelberht of Kent (bretwalda; Christian Frankish wife Bertha) allows preaching. Christ Church Canterbury; SS Peter and Paul monastery. Æthelberht probably baptized 601. Gregory wanted a metropolitan at *London*; politics keeps it at Canterbury. British bishops refuse Augustine’s authority (c. 603).
- Setbacks: Eadbald’s accession (616); East Saxon sons of Sæberht expel Mellitus; Rædwald keeps a pagan altar beside a Christian one. Edwin of Northumbria converts via Paulinus, then Hatfield Chase (633) rolls Bernicia/Deira back to heathenism.
- **Insular/Iona mission:** Oswald, converted in Dál Riata exile, requests monks; Aidan at Lindisfarne (634). Oswald interprets. Lindisfarne becomes the Insular centre (Aidan, Cuthbert).
- **Whitby 664:** Oswiu (Iona dating) vs Eanflæd (Kent/Roman dating) split the court calendar. Decision for Rome. Wilfrid ejects the Irish-trained community at Ripon.
- Monasteries as political technology: weregild can found houses (Minster-in-Thanet; Gilling after Oswine’s murder) that freeze blood-feud. Whitby under Hilda educates [[entities/caedmon]]’s world.
- Conversion of remaining pagan courts (Sussex, Wight) finishes in the 670s–680s. Official Christianity takes ~90 years; kingdoms often relapse after a first Christian king. By Bede’s later writing, clergy treat surviving “offerings to demons” as a fading problem.

Literacy, the Roman alphabet, and Latin loans in [[concepts/old-english]] ride this conversion. See [[concepts/anglo-saxon-paganism]] for what was being replaced.
