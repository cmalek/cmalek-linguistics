# Anglo-Saxon Kingdoms

Traditional “Heptarchy” map of [[entities/anglo-saxons|Anglo-Saxon]] England. These polities, not PIE branches, are why [[concepts/old-english-dialects]] are West Saxon, Mercian, Northumbrian, and Kentish.

## Key Ideas

| Kingdom | OE name | People | Span (clippings) | Fate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wessex | *Ƿestseaxna rīċe* | West Saxons | c. 519–927 | [[entities/alfred-the-great]] as *King of the Anglo-Saxons* from c. 886; Edward/Æthelstan conquer Danish kingdoms; Æthelstan takes York 927 as *King of the English* |
| Mercia | *Mercna rīċe* | Angles, Trent/Midlands | 527–918 | Independent to 879; client of Wessex 879–918. Peak “Mercian Supremacy” (Æthelbald, [[entities/offa-of-mercia]]) |
| Northumbria | *Norþhymbre* | Angles | 654–1066 | Height c. 700 (N. England + S. Scotland). After 867: split — Scandinavian York vs Anglian north; earldom under English overlordship later |
| Kent | *Cent rīċe* | Jutes | c. 455–825 | First Gregorian landing; client of Mercia in stretches; absorbed into Wessex 825 |
| Essex | *Ēastseaxna rīce* | East Saxons | 527–825 | Essex, Middlesex, much of Hertfordshire; last king Sigered cedes to Ecgberht of Wessex 825 |
| East Anglia | *Ēastengla rīċe* | East Angles | 6th c.–918 | Independent / Mercian client / [[concepts/danelaw]]; annexed by Wessex 918 |

Pasted maps (see [[references/visual-clippings]]): East Anglia with Ely, Beodericsworth, Rendlesham, Sutton Hoo, Devil’s Dyke; southern Britain with *Cantware*, *Wihtwara* / *Ytene sæte* as Jute, *Gewisse* around Winchester, mixed stripe toward Rochester/London.

Sussex (South Saxons) belongs to the same seven-kingdom scheme but has no dedicated clipping in this ingest. ^[inferred]

Kentish and West Saxon are the dialect baselines for the south; Mercian/Northumbrian are Anglian. Northumbria is also the home of [[entities/bede]] and [[entities/caedmon]]. Late West Saxon politics under [[entities/aethelred-the-unready]] (after [[entities/edward-the-martyr]]) is the setting of [[references/battle-of-maldon]].

[[entities/offa-of-mercia]] is the usual name for the high-water mark of eighth-century Mercia (coinage, Dyke, clash with Canterbury); Keynes: reputation, not a unity-of-England legacy.

By the time of [[entities/wulfstan-of-york]], West Saxon conquest of the [[concepts/danelaw]] has left one kingdom, and Æthelstan is remembered as king of the whole of England. *Polity* addresses that king, not seven. See [[concepts/kingdom-of-the-english]].

For ruler order, Bretwaldas, and the Mercian and Wessex overkingship phases, see the year-by-year chart on [[references/heptarchy-ruler-timeline]] (approximate positions, unsourced chart).

## The term “Heptarchy”

Per the Wikipedia-derived vault note, *Heptarchy* (Greek *hepta*, seven) was coined by the twelfth-century historian Henry of Huntingdon. The conventional seven are East Anglia, Essex, Kent, Mercia, Northumbria, Sussex and Wessex, but the number fluctuated and there was never a time when seven kingdoms each had one king; it is a label of convenience. By the eighth century the petty kingdoms gave way to four dominant ones: East Anglia, Mercia, Northumbria and Wessex. Essex, Kent and Sussex were absorbed entirely before unification. Hegemony shifted: Kent in the late 6th century, Northumbria and Wessex in the 7th, Mercia in the 8th (Offa).

Smaller polities alongside them: Bernicia and Deira (within Northumbria), Lindsey, the Hwicce, Magonsæte (a Mercian sub-kingdom in Herefordshire), Wihtwara (Jutes on the Isle of Wight), Middle Angles, Hæstingas, Gewisse, the Iclingas (precursor of Mercia), Meonwara, Middle Saxons, Pecsæte, Surrey, Tomsæte and Wreocensæte. Cited: Kirby, *The Earliest English Kings*; Keynes in the *Wiley Blackwell Encyclopedia of Anglo-Saxon England*; Stenton. The note also links the chart now at [[references/heptarchy-ruler-timeline]] and mentions the Burghal Hidage (Wessex's burhs). ^[inferred]
