# Norse Material Culture and Society

Distilled from the cultural essays of lessons 3-5 ([[skills/ut-eieol-old-norse]]); wider context in [[concepts/viking-age-expansion]].

## Ships (Gokstad, c. 900)
Best-preserved Viking ship: 76.5 ft long, 17.5 ft beam, ~3 ft draught, oak keel, clinker hull of 16 riven planks on 19 ribs; planks lashed below the waterline with spruce roots for flexibility. Speed came from narrow bow and wide flat midsection (mild hydroplaning) and light build; wool sail on a ~40 ft mast; starboard side-rudder with detachable tiller. Rigging is unknown; images suggest the ship could tack close to the wind.

## Women, marriage, children
Spirited, autonomous women were valued. Wives kept their patronymic, owned the bride-price and morning-gift, and could divorce as easily as husbands. Children could be exposed at birth for economic reasons; fosterage built alliances; illegitimate children had limited inheritance and could be adopted; legal adulthood at 12.

## Trade
Fur, iron, down and soapstone went out; honey, wheat, wine, cloth, glass and pottery came in. Markets: Kaupang (seasonal), Hedeby (year-round craft town), Birka (eastern route hub). Slaves, mostly Slavs, were sold east and south; the silver flow shifted to Germany as Middle Eastern mines were exhausted. Eastern Slavic records derive the name Rus from a Scandinavian group, though Arab sources do not distinguish Slav from Scandinavian. ^[ambiguous]
