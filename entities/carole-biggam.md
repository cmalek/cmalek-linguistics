# C. P. Biggam

C. P. Biggam, University of Glasgow, directed the Anglo-Saxon Plant-Name Survey (1999–2016). The statement read here is her 2013 introduction, [[sources/biggam-2013-plant-name-studies]], together with her Glasgow teaching unit on [[concepts/old-english|Old English]] plants.

She treats the plant lexicon as a folk classification filtered through monastic manuscripts. Regional names used by agricultural workers are largely lost. Some recorded English names are translations of Latin lemmata. Later English folk-naming, such as dozens of names for the foxglove, is her reason for thinking the Old English record is a small proportion of the names once in use. She also uses archaeology to show that a herbal text can name a plant’s role and still omit the rest of a treatment.
