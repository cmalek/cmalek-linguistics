# Otto Jespersen

Named the Great Vowel Shift in *A Modern English Grammar*, Part I (1909), as cited by [[sources/ikegashira-2020-stenbrenden-review|Ikegashira]]. Later writers treat his order as a drag chain: the long high vowels diphthongized first, and the other long vowels rose into the space they left. [[sources/prichard-2014-northern-gvs|Prichard]] says this conception became the better-known one. [[sources/stenbrenden-2016-long-vowel-shifts|Stenbrenden’s introduction]] quotes his reply to Luick: where /uː/ remained, /oː/ was not free to rise.

The 1909 grammar was not read for this page. The drag-chain position is as those later sources report it. See [[concepts/great-vowel-shift]] and [[entities/karl-luick]].
