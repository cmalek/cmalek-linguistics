# Ilse Wischer

Ilse Wischer (University of Potsdam) writes on grammaticalization of future markers in English and German. The papers that bear on Old English future time are a single project reported in stages.

- “Markers of futurity in Old English and the grammaticalization of *shall* and *will*,” *Studia Anglica Posnaniensia* 42 (2006): 165–178. [[references/wischer-2006-markers-of-futurity]]
- “Will and shall as markers of modality and/or futurity in Middle English,” *Folia Linguistica Historica* 29 (2008): 125–143. The abstract says *willan* and *sculan* were already auxiliaries in Old English, most often deontic, and asks how Middle English contexts let them be read as bare prediction. The article itself is paywalled; the abstract was read.
- With Gabriele Diewald, “Markers of futurity in Old High German and Old English,” in *Comparative Studies in Early Germanic Languages* (2013), 195–216. [[references/diewald-wischer-2013-futurity]]
- “On the use of *beon* and *wesan* in Old English,” in *English Historical Linguistics 2008* (2010), 217–235. The publisher abstract assigns current relevance to *wesan* and habitual or future reference to *bēon*, and argues for Insular Celtic influence. [[concepts/beon-and-wesan]]

She also has a 2019 paper, “Old English *wolde* and *sceolde*” (DOI `10.1075/cilt.346.06wis`), on the past forms in the Dictionary of Old English Corpus poetry. OpenAlex returned the abstract: past *willan* and *sculan* are less studied than the presents that feed the future, and she sorts poetic tokens by time reference, main-verb versus auxiliary use, and modality. The chapter was not read.
