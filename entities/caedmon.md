# Cædmon

Benedictine (fl. c. 657–684), cowherd at Streonæshalch (Whitby) under Abbess Hilda. Earliest English poet whose name is known. One of twelve Anglo-Saxon poets named in medieval sources, and one of three with both contemporary biography and surviving verse.

## Key Ideas

- Sole original life-source: [[entities/bede]]'s *Historia ecclesiastica*. Cædmon leaves a feast because he cannot sing; in a dream he is asked to sing *principium creaturarum* and produces a hymn to the Creator; Hilda's scholars test him; he takes vows and turns sacred history into vernacular verse.
- **Cædmon's Hymn** (West Saxon Alfredian prose of Bede, Book IV.25; Aerobics: MS during Alfred 871–876): dream-command *Sing mē frumsceaft*. Text:

  > Nū sculon heriġean heofonrīċes weard,  
  > Meotodes meahte ond his mōdġeþanc,  
  > weorc wuldorfæder, swā hē wundra ġehwæs,  
  > ēċe Drihten, ōr onstealde.  
  > Hē ǣrest sceōp eorðan bearnum  
  > heofon tō hrōfe, hāliġ Scyppend;  
  > þā middanġeard monncynnes weard,  
  > ēċe Drihten, æfter tēode  
  > fīrum foldan, Frēa ælmihtiġ.

  Composition estimated 658–680; 19 witnesses, all copies of the *Historia*. See [[concepts/history-of-english]] and [[concepts/christianity-in-anglo-saxon-england]] (Hilda’s Whitby).
- Bede’s OE narrative lists a lost corpus (Genesis, Exodus, Incarnation, Judgment, etc.) and a saint-like death: fourteen days’ mild illness, asks for the hospice, takes *hūsl* at midnight, waits for nocturns, signs himself, dies. Impersonal *ġedeofanade* (“[it] befitted [his] tongue to sing”) is the example on [[concepts/old-english-grammar]].
- Miracle details are not generally taken as literal; Cædmon's existence is not seriously doubted. Bede believed him important for English religious and intellectual life.
- Feast 11 February in several churches; Cædmon's Cross (1898) at St Mary's, Whitby — modern memorial (“fell asleep hard by 680”), not an Anglo-Saxon monument. EIEOL lesson 2 is Bede’s account.
