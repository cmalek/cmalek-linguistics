# Ælfric of Eynsham

Monk, homilist, and abbot, pupil of Æthelwold of Winchester, and the writer the Benedictine-reform survey calls the outstanding author of late West Saxon. He died about 1010. He is the other great vernacular author of [[entities/wulfstan-of-york]]’s generation.

## Why he matters for Polity

The three-orders paragraph in *Be cynestole* is taken from his letter to Sigeweard, almost word for word. Wulfstan puts *oratores* first. He also rewrites whole homilies of Ælfric into his own rhythm. Their programmes differ. Ælfric expects renewal from monastic example and from preaching. Wulfstan expects it from the king’s law and from bishops, and he does not privilege Benedictine life in the same way. ^[inferred]

That inference rests on the contrast already drawn in scholarship on the Blickling and Vercelli books, as quoted in a thesis extract read for this research: Ælfric’s reform comes through preaching and the monastic example; Wulfstan’s comes through royal power, without a special preference for Benedictine monasticism. The thesis was not read in full.

His Easter homily, paraphrasing Ratramnus, is the usual place a modern reader collides with eucharistic language. This page does not settle that collision. See [[concepts/late-anglo-saxon-christianity]].
