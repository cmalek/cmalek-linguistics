# Edward the Martyr

King of the English 8 July 975 – 18 March 978 (born c. 962). Eldest son of Edgar; mother probably Æthelflæd. House of Wessex.

## Key Ideas

- After Edgar’s death the succession was a faction fight: Dunstan and Æthelwine of East Anglia for Edward; queen Ælfthryth and Bishop Æthelwold for half-brother [[entities/aethelred-the-unready]]. Both children; Edward was chosen; Æthelred got the lands of the king’s eldest son.
- Edgar’s Benedictine land-grabs were reversed after 975 by legal action and some force (Ælfhere of Mercia vs Æthelwine), without open war.
- Murdered March 978 at Ælfthryth’s estate at Corfe, Dorset; buried hurriedly at Wareham; translated to Shaftesbury a year later. Pre-Conquest sources do not name the killer; almost all post-Conquest narratives blame Ælfthryth. Alternatives: Æthelred’s thegns acting without orders. ^[ambiguous]
- Sacrilege of killing a king shocked contemporaries; cult and 18 March feast. Historians note he was known for physical and verbal abuse; veneration is “thoroughly undeserved” in modern judgment.
- Evidence is thin (Roach): *Chronicle*, three authentic charters, Byrhtferth’s *Life of St Oswald*, later *Passio*.
