# Olaf Tryggvason

Óláfr Tryggvason later ruled Norway. English sources of Æthelred’s reign know a leader called Unlaf or Anlaf in the Viking attacks of the early 990s. Whether that man stood at Maldon in 991 is a separate question from his later career, and [[references/neubauer-2019-olaf-maldon|Neubauer]] argues that literary scholars have answered it too quickly.

The poem never names a Viking. Its spokesman is a *wicinga ār*. Neubauer’s abstract says Sveinn Forkbeard’s presence at this battle is disputed by most historians, and that Jóstein and Guðmund are otherwise obscure. Óláfr is then the only well-known candidate among proposed leaders, which is why he gets cast as the enemy of the poem. Neubauer calls that casting unreasonable and sets the poem against the Chronicle versions and the Latin histories.

The older vault note on [[references/battle-of-maldon]], from Glenn Foard (2003), already records that Chronicle E gives Olaf and 93 ships. That annal is one version. It is not a line in the poem. John of Worcester’s Justin and Guthmund, discussed in the WarHistory essay on the sources, may belong with the 994 treaty rather than with this fight. ^[ambiguous]
