# Karl Luick

Historical grammarian of English. [[sources/prichard-2014-northern-gvs|Prichard]] cites Luick (1896) for the push-chain: Middle English *ē* and *ō* rose first and displaced the high vowels. The argument she reports uses dialects that kept Middle English *ū* as a monophthong because *ō* had already been fronted. [[sources/stenbrenden-2016-long-vowel-shifts|Stenbrenden]] cites the same push-chain from *Historische Grammatik der englischen Sprache*, vol. 1 (1914–40), §479, and notes that Luick did not use the push-chain metaphor. [[sources/ikegashira-2020-stenbrenden-review|Ikegashira]] cites that grammar the same way.

Neither the 1896 study nor the *Grammatik* was read for this page. See [[concepts/great-vowel-shift]] and [[entities/otto-jespersen]].
