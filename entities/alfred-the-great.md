# Alfred the Great

Old English *Ælfrǣd* (c. 849 – 26 October 899). King of the West Saxons from 871, then *Anglosaxonum Rex* from c. 886. Youngest son of Æthelwulf; succeeded brothers Æthelbald, Æthelberht, and Æthelred. Life recorded by Asser.

## Key Ideas

- After years of Viking war, victory at Edington (878) leads to settlement with Guthrum, Guthrum's baptism, and the partition that becomes the [[concepts/danelaw]] (Scandinavian York, north-east Midlands, East Anglia vs Anglo-Saxon south and west).
- Reoccupies London (886) and styles himself king of all [[entities/anglo-saxons]] not under Viking rule.
- Military and legal reform; court school in English and Latin; translations including [[entities/bede]]'s *Ecclesiastical History*; begins the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle* (continued to 1154).
- Insists on educating in the vernacular. West Saxon of Winchester becomes the written koiné; that is why learners meet WS even though spoken Modern English is more Anglian. See [[concepts/old-english-dialects]] and [[concepts/old-english]].
- Epithet “the Great” appears from the thirteenth century and is popularized from the sixteenth; he is the only native English monarch routinely so labelled.
