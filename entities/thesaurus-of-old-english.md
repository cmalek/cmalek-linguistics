# A Thesaurus of [[concepts/old-english|Old English]]

*A Thesaurus of Old English* (Roberts, Kay, and Grundy, 1995; second edition 2000; online through the Historical Thesaurus) arranges attested Old English by concept rather than by spelling. [[sources/kay-2012-oe-semantics-lexicon|Kay]] counts about 34,000 word forms and 50,700 meanings in it. The distribution flags are described in [[sources/roberts-kay-toe-distribution]].

It was built from the standard dictionaries, especially [[entities/bosworth-toller]] and Clark Hall, to supply Old English material that the *Oxford English Dictionary* omits. It is a classification of recorded senses. An empty or thin category is a statement about those dictionaries and the surviving texts, which [[sources/kay-alexander-2015-thesauruses|Kay and Alexander]] say explicitly for terms of endearment. It is not a reconstruction of the words an Anglo-Saxon household would have needed.

The online edition is the usual way to browse a meaning class before asking whether a lifestyle domain looks under-lexicalized. Dubious place-name forms are flagged rather than mixed silently with attested prose words.
