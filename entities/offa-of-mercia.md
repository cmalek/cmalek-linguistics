# Offa of Mercia

King of [[concepts/anglo-saxon-kingdoms|Mercia]] 757–29 July 796. Son of Thingfrith, descendant of Eowa; took the throne after civil war following Æthelbald’s assassination, defeating Beornred.

## Key Ideas

- Early years: Hwicce and Magonsæte; overlord in unstable Kent; Sussex by 771 (later challenged). In the 780s Mercian supremacy over most of southern England: alliance with Beorhtric of Wessex (married Offa’s daughter Eadburh); East Anglia as overlord; Æthelberht II of East Anglia beheaded 794 (perhaps for rebellion).
- Christian king in conflict with Archbishop Jænberht of Canterbury. Persuaded Pope Adrian I to split Canterbury, creating an archdiocese of Lichfield so a willing archbishop could consecrate son Ecgfrith (787). Dispute with Worcester settled at Brentford (781).
- Coinage: high-quality portraits, some of queen **Cynethryth** (only Anglo-Saxon queen on a coin). Three surviving gold pieces; one copies a 774 Abbasid dinar with Arabic plus *Offa Rex* (alms or gifts to Rome?).
- Often called the most powerful Anglo-Saxon king before [[entities/alfred-the-great]]. Never ruled Northumbria except by marrying daughter Ælfflæd to Æthelred I (792). Simon Keynes: driven by lust for power, not a vision of English unity — reputation, not legacy. Ecgfrith lasted <5 months; Coenwulf followed.
- Sources: West Saxon *Chronicle* may underplay a Mercian; charters (subkings on witness lists); Offa's Dyke as resource-organisation; Alcuin’s letters at Charlemagne’s court; Tribal Hidage attribution disputed. [[entities/bede]] stops at 731, so is background only.
