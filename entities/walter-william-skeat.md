# Walter William Skeat

FBA (21 November 1835 – 6 October 1912). Anglican deacon and the leading British philologist of his generation; helped make English a university subject.

## Key Ideas

- Christ’s College, Cambridge; ordained 1860; curacies at East Dereham and Godalming; mathematics lecturer 1864–71, then English historical work.
- **Elrington and Bosworth Professor of Anglo-Saxon** at Cambridge from 1878. Finished Mitchell Kemble’s *Anglo-Saxon Gospels*; worked in [[concepts/old-english]] and Gothic. Best known for **Middle English**: standard Chaucer (six volumes 1894; *Chaucerian Pieces* 1897) and Langland’s *Piers Plowman*.
- *An Etymological Dictionary of the English Language* (1879–1882; enlarged 1910); hundreds of *Notes and Queries* etymology notes while compiling it.
- Founded and solely presided over the **English Dialect Society** (1873–96) to collect material for *The English Dialect Dictionary*; society dissolved 1897.
- Proposed a “woodpecker” (*biewolf*) etymology for the name Beowulf, criticised as violating [[concepts/germanic-sound-change|Grimm's Law]]; he may have recanted. See [[references/beowulf]].
