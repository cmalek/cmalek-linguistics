# Edmund the Martyr

King of the East Angles from about 855 until 20 November 869. Almost no contemporary narrative survives: Viking destruction of East Anglia erased charters. Coins (moneyers Dudda, Eadmund, Twicga shared with predecessor Æthelweard) imply a multi-year reign and a smooth accession.

## Key Ideas

- Death is in Asser and the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle*: killed in **869** after the Great Heathen Army moved into East Anglia. [[references/islendingabok]] dates Iceland’s settlement to the year Ívarr had “Eadmund inn Helgi” killed — **870** “as written in his story.” Treat 869/870 as the same horizon, not a second death. Later *passiones* disagree on battle-death vs capture. ^[ambiguous]
- Twelfth-century family/succession stories (e.g. *Annals of St Neots*: crowned aged fourteen at Burna, Christmas 856) have no known early source.
- Cult: memorial coinage around Wessex’s absorption of East Anglia (918); Abbo of Fleury’s *Passio* c. 986. Remains moved to Beodericsworth (Bury St Edmunds) in the tenth century; to London in 1010 for safety. With Edward the Confessor he was a patron saint of medieval England until George displaced them in the fifteenth century.
- Ælfric’s OE *Life* is now distilled on [[references/aelfric-life-of-edmund]]: Abbo via Dunstan; Hingwar/Hubba; hedgehog/Sebastian arrows; wolf and the speaking head *Her! Her! Her!*. Later *passiones* still disagree on battle-death vs capture. ^[ambiguous]
