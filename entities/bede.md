# Bede

Old English *Bēda* (c. 672/3 – 26 May 735), Benedictine of Monkwearmouth–Jarrow in [[concepts/old-english|Northumbrian]] Anglian territory. Best-known work: *Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum* (“Father of English History”). Wrote mostly in Latin; also a translator who made Greek and Latin Fathers accessible.

## Key Ideas

- Almost all biography is the last chapter of the *Historia* (finished c. 731; he implies his 59th year). Sent to Monkwearmouth at seven; survived the 686 plague with Abbot Ceolfrith.
- Popularized *Anno Domini* dating; worked on *computus* (Easter reckoning). Often ranked as the major scholar between Gregory the Great (d. 604) and Charlemagne (800).
- Origin story of the [[entities/anglo-saxons]] (Angles, Saxons, Jutes; invitation dated 450–455) is late relative to archaeology and should be read as eighth-century synthesis, not eyewitness. See [[concepts/history-of-english]].
- Sole narrative source for [[entities/caedmon]] and the context of Cædmon's Hymn.
- Name reflects WS *Bīeda* / Anglian *Bēda*, from *bēodan* “to bid, command.” Canonized and declared Doctor of the Church by Leo XIII (1899); shrine history at Durham.
