# Prototyping and moving toward construction

Chapter 12 treats prototypes as manifestations of design that stakeholders can inspect or interact with. A prototype deliberately emphasizes some characteristics and leaves others less developed. It can be a paper sketch, storyboard, simulation, physical mockup, or working software.

Conceptual design concerns what a product does and the concepts needed to use it. Concrete design concerns details such as layout, widgets, feedback, and appearance. The two evolve together as prototypes expose decisions and problems.

| Starting artifact | Prototype direction |
| --- | --- |
| Scenario | Break the interaction into storyboard scenes |
| Use case | Represent steps with an index-card interaction |
| Uncertain design choice | Build an alternative that makes the uncertainty testable |

The last row expresses a practical implication of the chapter's selective-prototype account. ^[inferred]

Construction progressively assembles the product using available components, physical-computing resources, or SDKs. A prototype's limitations need to remain visible when it is assessed. ^[inferred]
