# Six-Month Spoken Fluency

Chris Lonsdale’s 2013 TEDxLingnan talk (“How to learn any language in six months”) distilled through a working tutor’s 2026 reading. The claim is **freedom to speak**, not A0→C1 or exam scores.

## Key Ideas

**Two myths:** talent is the story told when the method fails (Zoe: failed Dutch, then Portuguese in six months with the same person). Immersion without structure is “expensive tourism”: a drowning man cannot learn to swim.

**Five principles:** (1) relevant content — greetings, apologies, “could you repeat that,” not weather-for-its-own-sake; (2) use the language as a communication tool from **day one** (speaking is a separate skill from grammar/writing); (3) [[concepts/comprehensible-input]] — understand the message, acquire the form; (4) physiological training — 43 facial muscles, ear vs eye; (5) psycho-physiological state — curiosity beats perfectionism.

**Seven actions:** brain soaking (lots of listening); meaning before words; mix 10 verbs × 10 nouns × 10 adjectives; core lexicon (~1,000 words ≈ 85% of daily talk, ~3,000 ≈ 98%); a “language parent” who does not mid-sentence correct (spouses are bad at this); copy the face; direct-connect words to images/feelings, not L1 glosses.

The 2026 overlay: AI voice modes as always-on language parent. That is the tutor’s inference, not Lonsdale 2013. ^[inferred]

This is a Modern-language method. Classroom [[concepts/old-english]] still needs grammar and [[skills/translating-ancient-texts]]; do not treat six-month Mandarin as a plan for reading [[references/beowulf]]. ^[inferred]
