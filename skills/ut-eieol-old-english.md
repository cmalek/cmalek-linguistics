# UT Old English Online (EIEOL)

University of Texas Linguistics Research Center *Old English Online* (Early Indo-European Online), series intro by Jonathan Slocum and Winfred P. Lehmann. Audio by Thomas M. Cable. Companion: Lehmann *A Grammar of Proto-Germanic*; LRC’s online [[entities/bosworth-toller]].

## Key Ideas

**Place in the family:** OE is West Germanic with Old Saxon, Old Frisian, Old Franconian, Old High German. Sisters: North Germanic (chiefly Old Norse) and East Germanic (Gothic). Proto-Germanic is reconstructed, not attested.

**Dialects (their three-way split):** West Saxon, Kentish, and **Anglian**. Four-way classroom maps ([[concepts/old-english-dialects]]) split Anglian into Mercian and Northumbrian. West Saxon is prominent because it is Alfred’s written language; most surviving literary texts are WS. Slocum dates Alfred 871–901; the wiki’s Alfred page uses death 899. ^[ambiguous]

**Spelling:** Latin alphabet from missionaries; never fully standardized; monks spell “phonetically,” so dialects look different. Alfred tried to regularize in the 9th c.; 11th-c. sound change breaks it again. Editors add macrons. Extra letters: runic **thorn** þ, later **eth** ð (no consistent [θ]/[ð] split — same word may use either); **ash** æ; **wynn** for /w/, replaced by *w* in modern editions. No silent letters.

**Lexicon teaser:** they claim >50% of the thousand most common OE words survive, >75% of the top hundred; >80% of the thousand most common MnE words come from OE. Examples: *cynn, hand, beran, cuman, fæst, ær, æfter, gif*. Treat the percentages as pedagogical, not a corpus study. ^[inferred]

**Syntax:** theoretically synthetic (endings carry role); in **prose** order is often close to MnE, with verbs sometimes clause-final and prepositions sometimes postposed. **Verse** is much freer. Their lessons “tend to translate Old English poetry as prose.”

**Inflection sketch:** nouns/adjectives/pronouns — case (incl. occasional instrumental), number (pronouns have dual), gender. Strong vs weak adjectives/nouns; some irregular classes they will not stress. Pronouns largely suppletive. Verbs: person, number, present/preterite, indicative/imperative/subjunctive (they add “perhaps optative”). **Seven** strong classes, **three** weak; plus preterite-presents and anomalous *be/do/go*. Numerals *two* and *three* may show gender.

**Lesson texts:** *Beowulf* prologue; Bede/Cædmon; Cynewulf and Cyneheard; Ohthere and Wulfstan; Alfred’s wars with the Danes; Maldon; *Genesis A* flood; *The Wanderer*; [[references/the-seafarer]]; *Beowulf* funeral. Sister series: [[skills/ut-eieol-old-norse]].
