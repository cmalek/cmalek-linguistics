# Planning an interaction-design evaluation

Chapter 14 organizes evaluation around why, what, where, and when to evaluate. Early informal feedback and planned studies serve different purposes; combinations of methods can provide a richer account.

| Setting and involvement | Main opportunity |
| --- | --- |
| Controlled setting with users | Observe specified tasks while limiting outside influences |
| Natural setting with users | Understand activity and experience in context |
| Evaluation without users present | Inspect or model likely problems |

Method choice affects the data and the conclusions it can support. Reliability, validity, ecological validity, bias, and scope need consideration when designing the study and interpreting its findings. Participants need information about the study and the treatment of their data.

[[skills/ux-usability-and-field-studies]] details controlled and natural user studies. [[skills/ux-inspections-and-models]] covers inspections, remotely recorded behavior, and prediction. [[skills/ux-data-gathering]] connects the plan to a practical recording procedure.
