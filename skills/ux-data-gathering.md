# Gathering evidence from users

Chapter 8 identifies five issues to settle before collecting data: goals, participants, the researcher–participant relationship, triangulation, and pilot studies. The choice of method follows the question and context.

## Choose the evidence

| Technique | Useful evidence | Main consideration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interview | Explanations, experiences, and reactions | Structure the questions to match the goal |
| Questionnaire | Comparable answers from a wider group | Wording and respondent motivation matter |
| Observation | Activity and context as they occur | Field work is complex and can affect behavior |
| Combined methods | Several perspectives on the same question | Plan how the evidence will be compared |

Interviews range from unstructured exploration to structured questions; focus groups introduce group discussion. Questionnaires can collect both numerical answers and free text. Observation may be direct or based on activity records, in natural or controlled settings.

Choose notes, photographs, audio, or video according to the detail needed, sensitivity, resources, and intrusiveness. Pilot the procedure before the main study.
