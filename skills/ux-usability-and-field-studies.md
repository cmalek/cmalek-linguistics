# Usability tests, experiments, and field studies

Chapter 15 distinguishes usability testing, experiments, and field studies. All can inform evaluation, but their questions and controls differ.

| Study | Principal focus |
| --- | --- |
| Usability test | Whether intended users can perform intended tasks and how they experience doing so |
| Experiment | A specified hypothesis about a relationship between variables |
| Field study | Use and experience in a natural setting |
| In-the-wild deployment | What happens when a technology is introduced into everyday activity |

Usability tests collect performance and reaction data on defined tasks. Experiments manipulate an independent variable and measure dependent variables while addressing other influences. Participation in one condition can affect performance in another, making the assignment and ordering of conditions consequential.

Natural settings contain interruptions and overlapping activities that controlled tests exclude. Some effects emerge only after people have used a tool long enough to become proficient. Choosing a setting therefore changes the phenomena that can be observed.

[[skills/ux-evaluation-planning]] establishes the question and limits of the study. [[skills/ux-data-analysis]] supports defensible interpretation. Results from one setting should not automatically be generalized to another. ^[inferred]
