## Regular forms

OE normally adds a simple *\-e* to the end of an **adjective** to make it an adverb.

| Adjective                  | Adverb                              |
| :------------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| ēċeliċ “eternal”           | ēċeliċe “eternally”                 |
| dēop “deep, profound”      | dēope “deeply, profoundly”          |
| fæst[^1] “fixed, firm”     | fæste “firmly”                      |
| lēas “false, lying”        | lēase “falsely, deceitfully”        |
| swīþ “strong, mighty”      | swīþe “strongly; exceedingly, very” |
| scearpliċ “sharp, painful” | scearpliċe “sharply, painfully”     |
| ætorht “bright”            | torhte “brightly                    |
| ān “one, single”           | āne “only”                          |

Note: an adjective with a case ending may look identical to an adverb.

* **Adjective:** *Swīþe rincas feallað.* \[The strong warriors are falling.\]  
* **Adverb:** *Rincas feohtaþ swīpe.* \[Warriors fight strongly.\]  
* **Adjective:** *Þæt torhte lond is wīd.* \[The bright land is broad.\]  
* **Adverb:** *He singeð torhte.*  \[He sings brightly.\]

##  Irregular forms

### Adjectives ending in *\-e*

The adjective/adverb relationship may also be obscured by some adjectives that end in  *\-e* naturally.

| Adjective           | Adverb                  |
| :------------------ | :---------------------- |
| clǣne “clean, pure” | clǣne “entirely”        |
| ēaþe “easy, kind”   | ēaþe “easily”           |
| þiċċe “thick”       | þiċċe “thickly”         |
| fæste “quick; firm” | fæste “quickly, firmly” |

### Adjectives requiring *\-līċe*

This is a move in the direction of the modern English *\-ly* ending for adverbs.

| Adjective       | Adverb              |
| :-------------- | :------------------ |
| blind “blind”   | blindliċe “blindly” |
| dīegol “secret” | dīegoliċe “secretly |

### Adverbs not from adjectives

Some adverbs not formed from adjectives may take other forms (many ending in *\-a*) but you can look them up easily 

* For example, *tela* (“well, properly”), *fela* (“very much, many), etc.

# Comparative and superlative forms

The vast majority of adverbs take *\-or* for their comparative and *\-ost* for their superlative forms.  Unlike adjectives, they take no other ending.

| Base      | Comparative | Superlative |
| :-------- | :---------- | :---------- |
| hearde    | heardor     | heardost    |
| dēope[^2] | dēopor      | dēopost     |

### Irregular forms

There are two groups of adverbs that form their comparatives and superlatives differently:

* The first group forms them from different roots  
* The second group forms the comparative by a change in internal vowel (i-mutation) rather than adding an *\-or.*

| Base           | Comparative | Superlative    |
| :------------- | :---------- | :------------- |
| lȳtle “little” | lǣs         | lǣst, lǣsest   |
| miċle “much”   | mā          | mǣst           |
| wel “well”     | bet, sēl    | betst, sēlest  |
| yfle “evilly”  | wiers       | wyrrest, wyrst |
| feor “far”     | fierr       | ferrest        |
| nēah “near”    | nȳr, nēar   | nīehst, nēxt   |
| sōfte “softly” | sēft        | sōftost        |

[^1]:  Why do we have *fæst* here, and then *fæste* in the irregular forms that means the same thing?

[^2]:  i-mutation refers to the history of this word in Proto-Germanic. Before the mutation, the word would have had an *\-i* ending, which transmuted to an internal vowel change.  All Germanic languages after proto-Germanic have this i-mutation implemented in different ways.
