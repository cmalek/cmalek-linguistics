# Grettis saga: Grettir and Glámr

Lesson 3 of [[skills/ut-eieol-old-norse]]. Essay: the Gokstad ship ([[concepts/norse-material-culture-and-society]]).

## The passage (ch. 35)

*Grettis saga* (14th c., author uncertain) tells of [[entities/grettir-asmundarson]], an outlaw said to have lived 996-1031. In the excerpt Grettir lies in a farmhouse hall waiting for the ghost-thrall Glámr.

**Beats.** Light burns; at a third of the night heavy noise on the roof; Glámr pokes his huge head through the door, stands up to the roof beam and peers in. He grips a cloak that covers Grettir; twice it holds, the third time it tears. Grettir seizes him round the waist; Glámr's strength breaks Grettir's grip; they wreck the hall and Glámr drags him toward the door. Grettir braces both feet against a stone in the doorway, Glámr falls backwards through the roof and out into the night.

**Beowulf link.** Many scholars see the saga and [[references/beowulf]] as variants of one story: descent into the unknown, a monster killed, arm severed, weapon retrieved; Glámr then the troll-wife parallels Grendel then his mother. The fight is a contest of strength rather than weapons and Glámr, like Grendel, is surprised by his opponent and wants to flee. The saga also gives one of the few clear descriptions of the one-room Viking Age house, later confirmed by archaeology.

## Language points

- **r-stems** (*faðir, móðir, bróðir, dóttir, systir*), **nd-stems** (*bóndi*) and other consonant stems (*maðr, nátt, tǫnn*) with i-umlaut plurals.
- **Demonstratives** *sá, sjá, hinn* and the postposed **definite article** (*-inn*), as in *skálanum*, *húsinum*.
- Strong and weak adjectives; possessives *minn, þinn, sinn*; past participle and supine (*hafa vegit*).
- Prepositions govern dative for position and accusative for motion.
