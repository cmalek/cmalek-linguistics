# van der Hoek 2010

Read in part (ch. 1 and the Anglo-Frisian section of ch. 4); the Dutch and German chapters were skimmed only.

- Penzl (1947): allophones [k] and [k'] split when i-umlaut and apocope left both before identical front vowels. van der Hoek, following Liberman, says no velar allophone could have stood before a front vowel, so the scenario "never happened".
- Chronology debate: Siebs, Luick and Campbell put palatalization in the continental period; Kluge argued it needed an inflectional *i* later apocopated. Hogg (1979), Stiles (1995) and Nielsen (2001) disagree; Fulk (1998) and Laker (2008) reply. No consensus on terminus ante quem.
- Siebs's list of Anglo-Frisian shared features includes assibilation of *k and *g.
- One example given for the ME reflex: OE *giefan* > ME *yiven*.

**Limits:** a minority theoretical position on allophones; the OE material is secondary.
