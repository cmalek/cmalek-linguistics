# Purgatory, conciliar history

Wikipedia, “Purgatory.” [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purgatory).

## Key claims

- Prayer that the dead may be purified is older than the Latin doctrine of purgatory, and is shared with other traditions.
- The Second Council of Lyon in 1274 was the first time the Catholic Church defined its teaching on purgatory. The Eastern Orthodox Church did not adopt that definition. Lyon did not call purgatory a third place or say that it contains fire. Florence (1431–1449) and Trent (4 December 1563) repeat the same restraint. Trent warns against speculations that do not edify.
- John Paul II and Benedict XVI have written that the word names a condition of existence, not a place.
- At Florence, Orthodox representatives rejected Latin elaborations while still holding that some of the saved are cleansed after death and are helped by the prayers of the living.

## Limits

This page is the later conciliar definition. It does not quote Ælfric or Wulfstan. What eleventh-century English Christians did for the dead has to be read from their own laws and homilies, then set beside this definition.
