# Wilcox 2000, The St. Brice's Day Massacre and Archbishop Wulfstan

Jonathan Wilcox, "The St. Brice's Day Massacre and Archbishop Wulfstan," in Diane Wolfthal, ed., *Peace and Negotiation: Strategies for Coexistence in the Middle Ages and the Renaissance* (Turnhout: Brepols, 2000), pp. 79–91. DOI [10.1484/M.ASMAR-EB.3.1345](https://doi.org/10.1484/M.ASMAR-EB.3.1345). Iowa's repository records the chapter and reprints the volume abstract, which is about peace as a positive ideal, not about Wulfstan. Brepols serves a preview page. The essay text was not obtained. Paper-search (Google Scholar, OpenAlex, Crossref) finds the chapter and the people who cite it.

## What later writers attribute to him

- [[references/savill-2021-st-brictius|Savill]] (2021), footnote 13, pairs this essay with Roach's *Æthelred*, pp. 186–251, as the case that the killings were a planned purge by reform-minded prelates on the witan, Wulfstan among them, newly archbishop in 1002. That sentence is Savill's bundling of the two works. ^[ambiguous]
- The same article, footnote 77, cites Wilcox pp. 84–85 for a contemporary concern with public legitimacy, and quotes him: "no sneaky business here."
- A Google Scholar snippet of Nicholas P. Schwartz, "Wulfstan the Forger: the 'Laws of Edward and Guthrum'," *Anglo-Saxon England* 47 (2018), says Wilcox suggested that, whatever Wulfstan's role in the execution may have been (if he had one at all), Wulfstan still... The sentence is cut off in the snippet. The article is paywalled, so the continuation is unknown here. The visible clause leaves a role in the killing itself uncertain. ^[ambiguous]
- Another Scholar snippet, from a paper on Æthelred's military displays, has Wilcox saying the order was aimed "at the settled population." That phrase alone does not say whether he means recent mercenaries who had settled or the older Danelaw. ^[ambiguous]
- An eNotes guide describes the essay as using the massacre to get at Wulfstan's views on reconciliation and peaceful coexistence in a Christian polity. That is a blurb, not the argument.

## What this does not settle

No fetched page quotes a sentence in which Wilcox says Wulfstan drafted, witnessed, or opposed the 1002 order. The secure link remains the date: he became archbishop of York and bishop of Worcester in 1002. See [[synthesis/Research: St Brice's Day Massacre]] and [[references/roach-2016-aethelred-massacre]].
