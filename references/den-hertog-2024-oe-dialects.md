# Subgrouping the Old English Dialects (den Hertog 2024)

Simon den Hertog, “Subgrouping the Old English Dialects Using Historical Glottometry,” *Amsterdamer Beiträge zur älteren Germanistik* 84, no. 4 (2024). The article tests whether Anglian is a real subgroup, and whether West Saxon and Kentish form another.

## The copy imbalance

Section 2.1 states that up to ninety percent of surviving Old English was written, though not necessarily composed, in West Saxon. Mercian, Northumbrian, and especially Kentish are sparse. He gives three causes: Alfred’s and Æthelwold’s quasi-standardization (Gneuss 1972), the prestige of the royal and Winchester dialect, and survivorship, because Viking attacks destroyed more northern libraries than southern ones.

That figure is a share of surviving written material, not a count of *ea* versus *æ* tokens. [[references/computational-study-of-old-english-2025|Martín Arista’s survey]] says West Saxon is about 60 percent of what survives. The two percentages do not match. ^[ambiguous]

## Two blocs

The same article is the source already cited for a coarser map: Anglian against a Saxo-Kentish bloc, with the four classroom dialects treated as partly prescriptive. The grammars still write rules at the finer grain. See [[concepts/old-english-dialects]].
