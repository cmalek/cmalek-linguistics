# Njáls saga: the attack on Gunnarr's hall

Lesson 4 of [[skills/ut-eieol-old-norse]]. Essay topic: women and children in Viking society ([[concepts/norse-material-culture-and-society]]).

## The passage (ch. 77)

*Brennu-Njáls saga* (c. 1250-1275, anonymous) contains three stories: Gunnarr's, [[entities/njal-thorgeirsson]]'s, and Kári's revenge. This excerpt is the climax of the first. Gunnarr, outlawed after refusing to leave Iceland, is attacked at home by Gizurr hvíti and men.

**Beats.**
- Description of the hall: timber, overlapping boards outside, windows by the ridge-beams with shutters; Gunnarr sleeps in a loft with his wife Hallgerðr and his mother.
- Þorgrímr austmaðr climbs the roof; Gunnarr thrusts his halberd (*atgeirr*) through the window; Þorgrímr falls and dies with a quip: find out yourselves, "but I found out his *spear* was home."
- Three assaults fail against Gunnarr's arrows. He shoots back an enemy arrow, wounding Eilífr Ǫnundarson; his mother warns against rousing them.
- Gizurr deduces from a gold-ringed hand that Gunnarr is short of arrows. Mǫrðr proposes burning him inside; Gizurr refuses and sarcastically invites the "clever" Mǫrðr to produce a workable plan.

The lesson stresses the dry humour and the natural, elliptical direct speech.

## Language points

- Contraction and old pronoun forms: *viti þér* for *vitið ér*; 3pl. forms for 1pl. (*skulu vér*), which also explains *brennu vér* "let us burn". ^[inferred]
- Relative **er** and *þá er* "when" (see [[concepts/old-norse-nouns-adjectives-and-articles]]).
- Strong vs. weak adjective as indefinite vs. definite (*Gizurr hvíti*, epithet in weak form).
- Present participle in *-andi*; *bóndi*, *frændi* as frozen participles.
- Subjunctive in indirect questions: *vissu þeir eigi hvárt Gunnarr myndi heim vera*.

## Cultural notes from the essay

Wives kept their own patronymic, held the bride-price and morning-gift, and could divorce by naming witnesses and a complaint; fosterage tied families together; boys became legal adults at 12 and often went raiding. Hervǫr, Angantýr's daughter, is the model warrior-woman.
