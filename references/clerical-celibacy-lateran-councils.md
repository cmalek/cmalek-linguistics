# Clerical celibacy in the Latin councils

Roman Curia, Congregation for the Clergy, “Priestly Celibacy in Patristics and Church History” (1 January 1993). [vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cclergy/documents/rc_con_cclergy_doc_01011993_chisto_en.html). An official historical survey, written to explain the Latin discipline.

## Key claims

- The First Lateran Council (1123), canon 7, makes the prohibition of cohabiting with wives into general law.
- The Second Lateran Council (1139), canon 7, following Pisa (1135), declares marriages contracted after ordination not only forbidden but non-existent (*matrimonium non esse*). The survey says this council is often misread as the first introduction of celibacy. Its own reading is that the council is tightening an older law of continence, and that later decretals still discuss men who had been ordained while married, according to the law of that time.
- The 1917 Code of Canon Law is a much later stage of the same discipline.

## Limits

This is a curial advocacy history. It is used here only for the dates and the wording of Lateran I and II. It does not describe Anglo-Saxon practice. For that, see [[references/english-benedictine-reform]].
