# Old English Grammar and Exercise Book (Smith 1896)

C. Alphonso Smith, *Old English Grammar and Exercise Book* (Louisiana State University, Baton Rouge, September 1896). Digital text: [oe_grammar.pdf](https://hcmc.uvic.ca/eol/oe/docs/oe_grammar.pdf), University of Victoria. Inflection is taken from Sievers; syntax and examples from Wülfing’s *Syntax in den Werken Alfreds des Grossen* (Bonn, 1894) and from Sweet, Bright, and Cook.

This is early handbook documentation, a generation before Mitchell. It already teaches the two-tense system.

## Key claims

- Early West Saxon had no distinctive future form. The present served as present proper and as future. Smith’s modern parallel is “I go home tomorrow” or “I am going home tomorrow” for “I shall go home tomorrow.”
- The forms *bēom*, *bist*, and the rest “are used chiefly as future tenses.” He adds that they survive in dialects and poetry, and that the Northern plural *aron* sits beside *sindon* and *sind*.
- *Ic wille* / *Ic wolde* and *Ic sceal* / *Ic sceolde* are listed with the anomalous verbs, beside *magan*, *cunnan*, *mōtan*, *bēon/wesan*, *dōn*, and *gān*. He calls the first group preterite-presents. He does not yet treat *willan* and *sculan* as a future conjugation.

## Limits

“Chiefly as future tenses” for the *b*-forms is stronger than later corpus work, which also finds habitual and generic *bēon*. The book is a drill grammar of Early West Saxon prose in Alfred’s orbit, not a survey of the whole corpus.
