# Reflexes of OE beon as a Marker of Futurity (Laing 2010)

Margaret Laing, “The reflexes of OE *beon* as a marker of futurity in early Middle English,” in Ursula Lenker, Judith Huber, and Robert Mailhammer (eds.), *English Historical Linguistics 2008*, vol. 1 (Amsterdam: John Benjamins, 2010), 237–254. Current Issues in Linguistic Theory 314. Publisher abstract: [chapter page](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/books/9789027287793-cilt.314.19lai). The LAEME publications list records the same citation.

This page rests on that abstract and the bibliographic record. The chapter body was not opened.

## Key claims

- Finite *b*-forms (*bēo, bist, bið*) were more likely than *s*-forms (*eom, eart, is*) in Old English contexts that involve futurity.
- That future use continues into Middle English.
- In the *Linguistic Atlas of Early Middle English* (LAEME), some text languages simplify the Old English split. In some, present indicative singular *b*-forms are restricted entirely to future senses.
- Where the *b*-root is already the normal present plural, that future restriction stays in the singular. In the North and parts of the North Midlands, where *ar-/*er-* plurals exist, the future *b*-system extends into the plural.
- The same volume’s Old English chapter is Ilse Wischer, “On the use of *beon* and *wesan* in Old English,” pp. 217–235. Its publisher abstract says *wesan* marks current relevance and *bēon* habitual or future events, and argues for Insular Celtic influence on the double paradigm.

## Limits

The three early Middle English subsystems are named in the abstract and not described here. ^[ambiguous] Wischer’s 2010 chapter was behind a script wall on the publisher site, so the Celtic claim is taken from that abstract alone.
