# DOE Corpus Cameron catalogue: identifiers and citation units

The six captured DOE catalogues map short titles and Cameron identifiers to editions and citation conventions. They are bibliographic records, not the texts themselves. Each entry identifies an edition or transcript and explains how a corpus citation is numbered.

## Catalogue divisions

| Prefix | Records in this capture | Catalogue category | Content |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| A | 202 | Verse | Verse |
| B | 2370 | Prose | Prose |
| C | 286 | Gloss | Glossed texts and occasional glosses |
| D | 150 | Gloss | Glossaries |
| E | 46 | Prose, Verse | Runic inscriptions |
| F | 61 | Prose, Verse | Non-runic inscriptions |

Counts refer to numbered records in these snapshots, not unique compositions, manuscript witnesses, words, or the size of a different corpus release. The fine-grained hierarchy matters: B1.1.1 is more specific than the B prose division.

## How to follow a citation

1. Keep the short title together with its full Cameron identifier.
2. Find the listed edition or DOE transcript.
3. Follow that record's citation rule: line, page and line, section, psalm and verse, or another stated unit.
4. Distinguish editorial numbering from numbering assigned by DOE.

For example, GenA,B is A1.1 and follows the edition's line numbering; Ælfric's English preface is B1.1.1 and follows page and line. The Alfred Jewel is F4, illustrating why an object can be a text-bearing corpus record. The E catalogue lists the Franks Casket as E2; missing identifier numbers should not be filled in by counting rows. ^[inferred]

[[entities/dictionary-of-old-english-corpus]] explains the corpus as a whole. [[references/beowulf]] and [[concepts/sermo-lupi-ad-anglos]] are textual contexts for using the index. [[skills/using-doec]] covers searching; a catalogue citation alone does not supply a lemma, inflectional class, or case frame. ^[inferred]

## Limits

The catalogues' broad `Text category` is not identical to every useful scholarly distinction: both runic and non-runic inscriptions appear as Prose here, and C and D both use Gloss. The archive preserves the full entry lists; this page distills how to use them rather than copying thousands of records.
