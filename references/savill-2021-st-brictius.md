# Savill 2021, Remembering St Brictius

Benjamin Savill, "Remembering St Brictius: Conspiracy, Violence and Liturgical Time in the Danish Massacre of 1002," *Journal of Ecclesiastical History* (2021), DOI [10.1017/S002204692100066X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S002204692100066X). The Cambridge PDF was readable on this pass. An earlier note in the vault had called it unreadable.

His own argument is that 13 November was chosen in part because of St Brice (Brictius): a ruler-victim of an unjust conspiracy, and a more obscure feast than Martinmas two days earlier, which Julia Barrow had already tied to the slaughtering season. That hypothesis is his, and he marks it as a hypothesis.

For the Wulfstan question he is a reporter. Footnote 13 sends the "planned purge by reform-minded prelates, Wulfstan among them" reading to [[references/wilcox-2000-st-brice-wulfstan|Wilcox 2000]] and to [[references/roach-2016-aethelred-massacre|Roach, *Æthelred*, pp. 186–251]]. Footnote 12, on the target being recently demobilized vikings who had settled after a truce that year, goes to Keynes, "The Massacre of St Brice's Day." Footnote 77 quotes Wilcox, pp. 84–85, "no sneaky business here," on the public character of the act.

The article therefore supports two separable claims. The liturgical dating is Savill's. The placement of Wulfstan inside the planning circle is a citation, and it is not a quotation from either book.
