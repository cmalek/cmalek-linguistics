# God's Lore and Worldly Law (Reinhard dissertation)

Ben Reinhard, *God’s Lore and Worldly Law: Archbishop Wulfstan, his manuscripts, and the Institutes of Polity* (Notre Dame dissertation; the public record used here is the abstract on the 2022 Figshare deposit, DOI [10.7274/s1784j05s8z](https://doi.org/10.7274/s1784j05s8z)). Found through paper-search (OpenAlex). The dissertation body was not read. His later articles on Cotton Nero A.i (*JEGP* 2020; Brill 2021) develop the manuscript side.

## Key claims

- Neglect of *Polity* follows from the lack of a usable modern edition. Reinhard’s remedy is to go back to the manuscripts rather than to Jost’s reconstructed text.
- *Polity* is not a free-standing treatise. It belongs to a wider project of social renewal, rooted in clerical and liturgical reform, and it looks toward the later eleventh-century clash of *regnum* and *sacerdotium*.
- The manuscript companions connect Wulfstan’s writing with two continental developments: the three orders of society, and the Peace of God. Reinhard reads *Polity* as a sub-Carolingian answer to the crises of the early eleventh century.
- He asks for a new edition, and then for literary study of the work once it is no longer treated only as a historical curiosity.

## Limits

Abstract only. “Looks forward” to the investiture clash is Reinhard’s framing of a text written before that clash became open war. ^[inferred] The abstract states the forward look; it does not demonstrate it in this extract.
