# Beowulf

> [!tldr] Longest and best-known [[concepts/old-english]] poem: 3,182 alliterative lines of Germanic heroic legend, unique manuscript Cotton Vitellius A. xv (Nowell Codex), c. 975–1025.

## Problem & Motivation

The poem is the central literary witness for OE poetic language — alliteration, variation, and kennings — and a standing problem for dating, dialect mixture, and pagan vs Christian composition.

## Method / Architecture

Unknown author (“the *Beowulf* poet”). Set in fifth–sixth-century pagan Scandinavia, not England. Plot: Beowulf of the Geats aids Hrothgar of the Danes; Heorot is emptied by Grendel for twelve years; Beowulf kills Grendel, then Grendel's mother; returns, later rules the Geats; fifty years on dies killing a dragon; cremation and barrow.

**Hero’s family (clipping):** son of Ecgþeow of the Swedish Wægmundings. Ecgþeow killed Heaðolaf of the **Wulfings** (*Wylfings* / ON *Ylfings*, “wolf clan”); weregild was too high; exile among the Danes; Hrothgar paid and took an oath. Hrothgar’s queen Wealhþeow is a Wulfing. The poet does not locate the clan; Norse sources make Ylfings rulers of the Eastern Geats (Östergötland). Helgi Hundingsbane is their most famous saga member. Sam Newton and others link the East Anglian **Wuffing** dynasty to the Wulfings and propose composition at that court (already noted as one possible English court below).

**Name *Bēowulf*:** several rival etymologies. Henry Sweet: “bee-wolf” / “bee-hunter” as a kenning for bear (also *Biuuuwulf* in the 1031 Durham *Liber Vitae*; Domesday *Beulf*). Andy Orchard (2005): “wolf of the god Beow,” parallel to ON *Þórólfr*. [[entities/walter-william-skeat]]: Dutch *biewolf* “woodpecker”; criticised as violating [[concepts/germanic-sound-change|Grimm's Law]]; he may have recanted. Bosworth: variant of *beado-wulf* “war-wolf,” compared to Icelandic *Bodulfr*; Sarrazin’s *Böðvarr* path was questioned by Bugge. Leonard Neidorf: seventh-century *Biuuulf* uses an unproductive *Beow-* element, so the hero likely existed in lost legend before the extant poem. ^[ambiguous]

Two scribes; mostly Late West Saxon with other dialect forms, implying long transmission across [[concepts/old-english-dialects|dialect areas]]. *Judith* shares the manuscript (binding order has shifted). Fire at Ashburnham House (1731) charred margins. First printed edition Thorkelin 1815.

Kennings and synonymy (History of English clipping): 36 words for hero, 20 for man, 12 for battle, 11 for ship; *hronrad* “whale-road” = sea; 903 compound nouns, 578 hapax in-poem, 518 unique to this poem.

Hygelac's Frisian raid is independently dated c. 521 via Gregory of Tours. Lejre excavations found mid-sixth-century halls matching Heorot's traditional site.

## Limitations

Hero Beowulf himself is unattested elsewhere. Liuzza: the poem is “frustratingly ambivalent,” legendary history on a roughly recognizable Scandinavian map.
