# Íslendingabók

EIEOL lesson 1 prints the opening of the surviving *Libellus* (also called *Íslendingabók*). Do not confuse with Ari’s lost longer book of genealogies and kings’ lives.

## Problem & Motivation

Classroom ON prose with explicit source-naming. Anchors Icelandic settlement to a date already on [[entities/edmund-the-martyr]].

## Method / Architecture

**Frame:** first version for bishops Þorlákr and Ketill and priest Sæmundr; second version drops *Ættar-tala* and *Konunga-ævi*, adds what later became clearer; false statements should yield to what proves truer.

**Settlement chapter (distilled):** Iceland first settled from Norway in the days of Haraldr inn Hárfagri, son of Hálfdan inn Svarti, at the time Ívarr Ragnarsson Loðbrókar had Eadmund inn Helgi, king of the English, killed — **870** after Christ’s birth, “as written in his story.” Ingólfr, a Norwegian, first voyage when Haraldr was 16, second a few years later; settled south in Reykjavík; Ingólfshǫfði first landfall; Ingólfsfell later possession. Iceland then wooded between mountain and shore. Christian *papar* (Irish books, bells, croziers) leave rather than live with heathens. Mass emigration until Haraldr bans it as depopulation; tax five ounces, later *landaurar*; Óláfr inn Digri sets a half-mark except women and those he exempts. Witness: Þorkell Gellisson.

## Limitations

Ari’s 870–Edmund synchronism is a learned date, not an independent English chronicle. Irish hermits vs later nationalist readings of an “empty” island: he reports *papar* then departure. ^[ambiguous]
