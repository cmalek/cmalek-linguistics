# Fourth Lateran Council, 1215, English canons

English translation of the constitutions of the Fourth Lateran Council (November 1215), from Documenta Catholica Omnia. The council met under Innocent III. About 404 bishops were present. No Greek bishops attended, apart from the Maronite patriarch and a legate of Alexandria.

## Key claims

- Canon 1, the confession of faith, teaches one God in three persons, creation from nothing, and the fall of the devil and of man. Christ’s body and blood “are truly contained in the sacrament of the altar under the forms of bread and wine, the bread and wine having been changed in substance, by God’s power, into his body and blood.” Only a properly ordained priest confects that sacrament. Baptism in water in the name of the Trinity saves children and adults. After baptism, restoration is by true penitence. Married people, as well as virgins and the continent, can be saved.
- The same canon cites Peter Lombard on the Trinity (against Joachim of Fiore). It does not list seven sacraments.
- Canon 5 states that the Roman church has “a primacy of ordinary power” over all other churches, and that patriarchs receive the pallium from the Roman pontiff and swear fidelity and obedience.
- Canon 14 presses clerical continence, and still speaks of clerics who, “following the custom of their region,” have not renounced the marriage bond. Those men are to be punished more severely if they fall into sin, because lawful marriage was open to them.
- Canon 18 forbids a cleric to bless a purgation by ordeal of water or hot iron, and forbids a cleric to pronounce a sentence of blood.
- Canon 21 requires every faithful person past the age of discernment to confess all their sins to their own priest at least once a year, and to receive the eucharist at least at Easter, unless their priest advises abstaining. The priest may not reveal a confessed sin.

## Limits

This is a translation, not the Latin acta. The English rendering “changed in substance” is the translator’s phrase for the council’s eucharistic sentence. The file’s URL contains commas; the copy read for this page is the English canon text hosted by Documenta Catholica Omnia.
