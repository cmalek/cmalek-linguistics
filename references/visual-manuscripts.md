# Manuscripts, documentary pages, and illustrated texts

The images show decorated initials, reader and writer portraits, book-presentation scenes, documentary subscriptions, printed title pages, and narrative illustrations. These visual forms connect written texts to their material presentation, but they do not by themselves identify a manuscript shelfmark or date. ^[inferred]

A page begins “Sermo Lupi ad Anglos”; another printed page is headed “OPERA BEDAE VENERABILIS.” Those visible strings are extracted labels, not proof of a particular edition. The arrow-martyrdom and beheading scenes suggest an Edmund narrative cluster; exact manuscript identifications remain unresolved. ^[inferred]

This is a visual reading index, not a diplomatic transcription. Handwriting, abbreviations, small subscriptions, Hebrew text, and coin-like letterforms require separate close reading before linguistic claims can be made.
