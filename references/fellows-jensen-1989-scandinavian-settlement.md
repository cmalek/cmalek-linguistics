# Scandinavian Settlement in England (Fellows-Jensen 1989)

Gillian Fellows-Jensen, “Scandinavian Settlement in England: the evidence of place-names and personal names,” *Actes des congrès de la Société d’archéologie médiévale* 2 (1989): 77–94. [Persée](https://www.persee.fr/doc/acsam_0000-0000_1989_act_2_1_1024).

She uses A. H. Smith’s map of parish names of Scandinavian origin (*English Place-Name Elements*, 1956, map 10). Very few of those names fall south and west of the boundary in the treaty of Alfred and Guthrum, which she quotes from Cambridge, Corpus Christi College 383: up the Thames, up the Lea to its source, straight to Bedford, up the Ouse to Watling Street. The North and East Ridings are dense, and so are Lincolnshire and Leicestershire. East Anglia is weaker, with several names in the Norfolk and Suffolk Broads. Essex, Cambridgeshire, and Hertfordshire are comparatively empty, which she calls surprising given where the boundary runs.

No count per square kilometre is in the article. “Very few” south and west of the treaty line, against concentrations in Yorkshire and the east Midlands, is the comparison. [[references/purkiss-2025-limits-of-the-danelaw|Purkiss]] warns that continuing that line along Watling Street toward Chester is a later reading of the treaty, not the treaty’s words.
