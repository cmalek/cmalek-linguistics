# Old English articulation and rune charts

The articulation chart groups consonant symbols beneath the labels “Bilabial,” “Labiodental,” “Dental,” “Alveolar,” “Postalveolar,” “Palatal,” and “Velar.” It relates them to a side-view diagram of lips and tongue positions, using examples such as Preost, Biscop, Mann, Wif, Fæder, Heofan, and Þæt.

Its symbol row includes modern IPA values and learner examples. It should be read as a teaching chart rather than evidence that every displayed sound was a separate Old English phoneme in every period and dialect. ^[inferred]

The rune chart pairs glyphs with transliterations, names, and English glosses. Readable entries include “feoh - f / wealth,” “þorn - þ / thorn,” “gyfu - g / gift,” “wynn - p / joy,” “dæg - d / day,” and “stan - st / stone.” The printed wynn transliteration resembles p; this is recorded as a visual ambiguity, not adopted as a transcription rule. ^[ambiguous]

The grid is not a dated inscription. A rune chart can support character recognition without establishing the spelling conventions or inventory of an individual manuscript or object. ^[inferred]
