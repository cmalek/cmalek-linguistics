# Old English Online: Beon and Wesan

[Old English Online](https://oldenglish.info/irgv2.html), irregular verbs, “Beon and Wesan.” A classroom page with paradigms and two worked sentences. It is the sort of documentation a learner meets before Mitchell or the Helsinki Corpus.

## Key claims

- *Wesan* and *bēon* are two present stems of one suppletive verb, with one shared past (*wæs*, *wǣre*, *wǣron*).
- *Wesan* is the ordinary present: *hē is wiga* “he is a warrior.”
- *Bēon* is for habitual or “gnomic” truths: *wyrd biþ ful āræd*, *fugolas bēoþ cafe*, *wiga biþ strang*.
- “While there is technically no future tense in Old English, the verb *beon* is used when expressing the future tense,” with *iċ bēo gearo sōna* “I will be ready soon.”
- The paradigm they print is *bēo, bist, biþ, bēoþ* beside *eom, eart, is, sind*.

The same site’s irregular-verb sheet says *willan* is ‘to want’, and that ‘will’ is the translation to try last.

## Limits

“Used when expressing the future” is broader than the corpus result that *b*-forms are preferred in future contexts and also occur in generic ones. The page gives no source for the gnomic examples beyond the sentences themselves.
