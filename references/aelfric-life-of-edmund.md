# Ælfric's Life of St Edmund

> [!tldr] Homiletic saint’s life: Abbo → Dunstan → Ælfric. Pious East-Anglian king vs Hingwar and Hubba (869); hedgehog-like martyrdom; wolf and “Her! Her! Her!”

## Problem & Motivation

Mitchell & Robinson print Cotton Julius E.vii (spellings normalized). Malek’s facing PDF is the first vault copy with actual OE+English (the old `_raw` Markdown was empty).

## Method / Architecture

**Frame:** Abbo of Fleury hears Dunstan retell the swordbearer’s story to Æthelstan; writes Latin *Passio*; Ælfric turns it into English. Not a mechanical translation: shorter speeches, less rhetoric, then a shift into **alliterative prose** (four-stress units, alliteration, without verse vocabulary or strict metre). Editors often lineate it like poetry.

**Plot distilled:** Edmund *eadmod*, *anræd*, generous to widows. Danish fleet under Hingwar and Hubba (devil-joined) wastes Northumbria; Hingwar rows to East Anglia the year Alfred is 21. Messenger: submit to *manræden* or die. Edmund will not forsake Christ or fight (Peter’s sword). Bound, scourged at a tree, shot *swa swa igles byrsta* like Sebastian, then beheaded still calling on Christ. Head hidden in brambles; people search crying *Hwær eart þu nu, gefera?*; head answers *Her!*; grey wolf holds it and will not eat it (inversion of the raven/wolf/eagle battlefield trio). Body later found incorrupt; miracles; English saints (Cuthbert, Æthelthryth) as local proof of faith.

**Classroom notes:** *welwillendnysse* as calque of *benevolentia*; dative absolute; ethical dative; *wōdlice* sharing *wōd-* with Woden. Anti-Jewish closing is Ælfric’s homiletic polemic, not a historical claim about 869. ^[inferred]
