# The Status of Passive Constructions in Old English (Jones and Macleod 2017)

Howard Jones and Morgan Macleod, “The status of passive constructions in Old English,” February 2017. Oxford University Research Archive PDF: [Jones and Macleod](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:0671ac99-83c2-485d-97a1-1eb0b2f71e43/files/m0fea92eabff8a24aa024a32a98c3b7cd). The sample is a slice of the York-Toronto-Helsinki parsed prose corpus: *Cura Pastoralis*, Bede, Orosius, both versions of Gregory’s Dialogues Book I, parts of Ælfric’s Old Testament, the Benedictine Rule, Wulfstan’s Homilies, and Chronicle MS E from 966 to 1121. Latin sources are used to disambiguate where they exist.

They treat *wesan* and *bēon* together against *weorðan*. Both “be” verbs lack an inherent transition. *Bēon*’s older “become” root, they say, had already become “be,” with a perfective flavour that some authors (Ringe and Taylor 2014) set against imperfective *wesan*.

## Key claims

- A passive here is copula plus participle of a transitive verb, patient as subject. They do not assume the combination is grammaticalized. Where *wesan* plus participle can be read as an intransitive perfect, they keep it only if a transitive reading is tenable.
- The compositional prediction: *weorðan* requires a transition; *wesan/bēon* does not. Both may express a transitional event. Only *wesan/bēon* may express a state.
- The counts match that prediction. Non-transitional states: *weorðan* 0, *wesan/bēon* 285. A second non-transitional class: *weorðan* 0, *wesan/bēon* 134. Telic events: *weorðan* 311 (27.4%), *wesan/bēon* 824 (72.6%). Ingressives: *weorðan* 5 of 7. Ambiguous tokens are almost all *wesan/bēon* (374 against 6), which fits a copula that allows both readings.
- Kilpiö (1989: 67, 85), on Bede and the Pastoral Care, had already found *weorðan* only in actional passives. Mitchell (1985, I, 324–33) cited near-identical eventive pairs and some apparent stative *weorðan*. Jones and Macleod re-read Mitchell’s stative examples as events (the prison becoming filled; the siege beginning, with *swā lange* covering the time after).
- They find no non-compositional passive meaning, so no grammaticalization in their narrow sense (the copula bleached inside the construction, as later German *werden* is). *Wesan* is simply the unmarked choice inside the range the semantics allow. German, on their earlier work, did specialize: *sein* passives became stative only, *werden* passives eventive.

## Limits

*Bēon* and *wesan* are collapsed for the counts. The sample prefers translations from Latin, with two late vernacular controls. Poetic passives are out.
