# STELLA Essentials of Old English: the Verb Phrase

University of Glasgow STELLA course, “Essentials of Old English,” section “The Verb Phrase” (§§32–50): [plus-verb-phrase.html](https://stella.glasgow.ac.uk/eoe/plus-verb-phrase.html). This is a teaching grammar, not a corpus study. It is useful because it states the classroom rule in the same terms as Mitchell and Robinson.

## Key claims

- Finite verbs have special forms for present and preterite (§37). Mood is a separate set of forms: indicative, subjunctive, imperative (§§38–41).
- Future time, perfect aspect, and passive can be auxiliary plus a non-finite verb, as in Present-Day English (§42).
- *Willan* + infinitive “seems to have been used towards the end of the OE period” for future time. The ordinary sense is volition: *iċ wille gān* is “I want to go.” Future time is more often the simple present. When the verb is ‘be’, the *bēon* forms tend to be used rather than *eom*, *is*, and the rest (§44).
- *Wesan/bēon* or *weorþan* plus past participle, and *habban* plus past participle, are what Mitchell and Robinson (1992) call resolved tenses, against the simple present and preterite (§46). STELLA assigns those resolved forms to perfect and passive, not to a future paradigm (§§47–48).
- Simple present and preterite, sometimes with an adverb (*iċ lufode ǣr* “I had loved”), are more frequent than auxiliary phrases (§49).

## Limits

The page gives no counts and no edition for the examples. “Towards the end of the OE period” for future *willan* is looser than Wischer’s claim that bleached prediction is already in Alfredian prose. ^[ambiguous]
