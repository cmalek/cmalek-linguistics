# Halbrooks 2003, praise and blame in Maldon

John Halbrooks, “Byrhtnoth’s Great-Hearted Mirth, or Praise and Blame in *The Battle of Maldon*” (2003), Dialnet record [1368769](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=1368769). This page uses the OpenAlex abstract. The article was not read past that abstract.

Since Tolkien’s *The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth Beorhthelm’s Son*, Halbrooks says, commentary has often shrunk to *ofermod*. The gloss decides whether Byrhtnoth is praised or blamed. He argues that the ambiguity is the poet’s design. The word, and the poem, push toward heroic elegy and toward Christian admonishment together. It is a sign of praise and of blame. Two contradictory pictures of the hero run through the preparation and the fighting, and the poet tries to hold them in one work. He sets a skaldic analogue, *Hákonarmál*, beside the Old English lines.

He quotes the crux as: *ða se eorl ongan for his ofermode alyfan landes to fela laþere þeode*, “Then the earl, because of his great-hearted pride, began to give too much land to those hostile people.” Tolkien’s version in the abstract is: the earl in his overmastering pride yielded ground to the enemy, as he should not have done.

## Limitations

The abstract breaks off inside the Tolkien comparison. The *Hákonarmál* parallel is named and not demonstrated here. I. J. Kirby, “In Defence of Byrhtnoth,” *Florilegium* 11 (1992), argues from its own abstract that censure of Byrhtnoth is a mistake. That article was not read. It belongs with the defence side of the same debate on [[concepts/ofermod]].
