# Insley 2026, Wigelm or Wigelin at Maldon line 300

John Insley, “The Battle of Maldon 300: Wigelm or Wigelin(e)?” *Anglia* (2026), DOI [10.1515/ang-2026-0015](https://doi.org/10.1515/ang-2026-0015). Abstract only. The article was not read.

The transcript, made about 1724, reads *wigelines bearn* at line 300. The manuscript was largely destroyed in the Cotton fire of 1731, so the reading cannot be checked against the leaf. Insley asks whether the letters are accurate, and who Wigelin(e) is. He emends *wigelines* to *wigelmes*, Old English *Wīghelm*. He rejects a feminine *Wigelin(e)* as the mother of Wistan, Thurstan’s son (lines 297–98). His positive claim: Wistan Thurstan’s son is the anglicized name of a Scandinavian who took the Old English name *Wīghelm* at baptism on entering Ealdorman Byrhtnoth’s household.

How one man carries both “Thurstan’s son” and “Wighelm’s son,” or how Wistan and Wighelm are the same baptismal identity, is the argument of the unread article. The abstract states the conclusion. It does not set out the name equation. Treat the identification as his proposal. See [[concepts/people-named-in-maldon]].

Teaching texts often print *Wīgelmes bearn* already, as the [Old English Aerobics](https://www.oldenglishaerobics.net/maldon.php) text does. That print is an editorial choice sitting on top of the transcript Insley cites.
