# Maps of kingdoms, migrations, and literary geography

The collection includes settlement maps with arrows, territorial reconstructions, maps of dioceses, place-name distributions, and a map explicitly labelled with Beowulf’s voyage. These serve different purposes and should not be collapsed into one undated map of “Anglo-Saxon England.” ^[inferred]

Visible titles distinguish Britain 400–500 CE, Britain about 600, the Heptarchy, English dioceses 850–925 and 950–1035, and the Midlands c. 912. Repeated map designs are preserved as separate snapshots; repetition is not independent corroboration. Exact borders, route chronologies, and small place labels were not independently verified.

## Context

[[concepts/anglo-saxon-kingdoms]], [[concepts/danelaw]], and [[references/beowulf]] provide the historical and literary contexts. See [[references/anglo-saxon-visual-source-guide]] for the collection-wide evidence limits.
