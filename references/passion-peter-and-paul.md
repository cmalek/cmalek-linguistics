# Passion of the Apostles Peter and Paul

> [!tldr] Old English homily headed *III Kalendas Iulii* (29 June): gospel of Peter’s confession, Bede as *trahtnere*, then an apocryphal Roman *passio* against Simon the magician.

## Problem & Motivation

Vernacular preaching of apostolic authority (*stænen* / keys of heaven) and a colourful anti-sorcery narrative for a feast day.

## Method / Architecture

**Gospel block:** Matthew at Caesarea Philippi. Peter: *Þu eart Crist, ðæs lifigendan Godes Sunu.* Christ: *stænen*, church on this stone, hell-gates, keys, bind/loose. [[entities/bede]] explains the place-name (Philip the tetrarch + Tiberius). False identifications (John, Elijah, Jeremiah) vs living God vs dead giants and heavenly bodies as idols.

Keys are not metal but *anweald*; later granted to all apostles at the insufflation of the Spirit, and to bishops/priests who keep gospel care — Peter’s key is singled out so schism from his confession loses forgiveness and heaven.

**Passio:** after the Ascension Peter preaches in Asia Minor, then 25 years at Rome. Rival **Simon** Magus claims to be Christ. Widow’s-son contest: Simon makes the corpse stir; Peter demands speech, standing, food; raises the boy. Simon siccs a huge hound; Peter forbids biting, the dog shreds clothes. Nero befriends Simon; Christ promises Paul as ally. Paul arrives; seven months of preaching; Nero’s household converts (including, in this telling, Nero’s wife Libia and Agrippina). Simon’s bronze serpent, levitating, shape-shifting before Nero; Peter heals and raises. Thought-reading contest with blessed bread vs phantom dogs.

**Close of the passio (marked teaching text):** Simon has a high tower built; he flies; Peter and Paul pray; demons drop him; he bursts into four pieces that become four witness-stones. Nero jails the apostles, hoping Simon will rise on the third day; Peter says he will not. Paul is beheaded; Peter asks to be crucified **head-down**. Latin aside: Jerome and others say they died on the same day, in the 36th year after Christ’s passion. Nero, hated, flees to a wood and is said to be torn by wolves. Greeks try to carry the bodies east; an earthquake; relics held at *Catacumbas* for a year and a half.

Canonical **Simon Magus** (Acts 8) is the Samaritan who offers money for the Spirit — *simony*. Later heresiology (Justin, Irenaeus, Hippolytus) and apocrypha (*Acts of Peter*) expand him into a Gnostic founder and Peter’s flying rival at Rome. The homily’s Nero-Rome magician is that apocryphal type, not Acts 8. Whether the historical Simon was a Gnostic or only labelled one is disputed. ^[ambiguous]
