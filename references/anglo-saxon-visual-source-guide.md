# Anglo-Saxon visual sources: a guide to the image collection

This collection combines historical maps, photographed objects, manuscripts, buildings, later artwork, and two language charts. Most files have only a timestamped paste name. Their contents can be described, but their original attribution and context have generally not survived with them.

## How to use the collection

A map is a reconstruction with a particular date and purpose. A museum photograph needs an object record. A manuscript image needs its shelfmark and folio. A later painting or memorial is evidence of representation and commemoration, not a contemporary likeness. These distinctions guide which claims a picture can support. ^[inferred]

[[concepts/anglo-saxon-kingdoms]], [[concepts/christianity-in-anglo-saxon-england]], [[references/beowulf]], and [[references/judith]] supply existing topics for comparison. The group pages suggest connections without asserting unknown source identities.

## Coverage and unresolved evidence

The collection was visually surveyed in contact sheets, with full-size checks of the articulation chart, rune chart, Sermo Lupi page, Alfred plaque, and black image. Descriptions are selective observations, not complete paleographic, epigraphic, art-historical, or cartographic analysis. Repeated designs remain separate files and do not raise confidence.
