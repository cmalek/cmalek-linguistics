# East–West Schism

Wikipedia, “East–West Schism.” [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East–West_Schism).

## Key claims

- The article dates the schism from 16 July 1054, the mutual excommunications associated with the legation of Humbert of Silva Candida to Constantinople.
- It also treats 1054 as one act in a longer estrangement, not as the moment every Christian noticed a new denomination. In 1965 Paul VI and Athenagoras I nullified the anathemas.
- Theological friction over the *filioque*, papal authority, and leavened or unleavened bread is older than 1054.

## Limits

Nothing in this article is about Worcester or York. The date matters because [[entities/wulfstan-of-york]] died in 1023, and Junius 121 was compiled about 1060–1072. England’s books stay Latin on both sides of 1054.
