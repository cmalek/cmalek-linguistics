# Old English scholarship 2016–2026 — scoping review protocol and search record

## Objective and author decisions

Map the topics and methodologies represented in academic research directly concerning Old English from **January 1, 2016, through October 28, 2026**. The purpose is orientation for an Old English student, rather than an evaluation of which interpretation or method is best.

The user confirmed “yes, whole field, 2016 through today,” selected “Articles and chapters; books noted separately (recommended),” and then selected “Scoping review.” The user subsequently confirmed: “i think abstracts work well as long as they give the topic and methodology.” Abstracts are therefore an authorized classification basis when both dimensions are described; missing information must remain unspecified or provisional. These decisions close the scope and review-form questions for this run. The date range covers parts or all of eleven calendar years; it is not a rolling ten-year interval.

## Review frame

The units are scholarly publications; the concept is their substantive topics and research approaches; the context is Old English studies across linguistic, literary, material, historical, educational, and reception research. This adapts the population/concept/context approach to a humanities publication map. Eligibility and source types should align with the review objective. [JBI eligibility guidance](https://jbi-global.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/MANUAL/pages/355862707/10.2.4+Inclusion+criteria).

**Status:** a web-based scoping-review draft with an explicitly bounded retained corpus. Preliminary discovery began before the author chose the review form and before this protocol was written. This is a retrospective record of those decisions and a rule set for the subsequent gap searches, not a preregistered protocol. The work does not claim full PRISMA-ScR compliance. [PRISMA-ScR](https://www.prisma-statement.org/scoping) is used as a reporting reference.

## Eligibility

Include journal research/review articles, scholarly notes, and scholarly book chapters with a substantive Old English connection. Include literary and linguistic scholarship, manuscripts, source study, translation, intellectual history, law, medicine, runes, cultural criticism, modern reception, and teaching. Include work on later copying and reception of Old English; the publication date, not the historical period studied, governs eligibility.

Record major monographs and editions separately. Keep conference proceedings, digital resources, and biomedical reconstructions derived from Old English medicine in clearly marked supplementary groups. General medieval archaeology/history without a direct connection to Old English evidence, popular articles, book reviews, dissertations, unverified titles, and publications not available by the cutoff do not enter the main corpus. Bibliographic annual reviews inform the coverage check but do not count as substantive research records in the main corpus.

The original issue/publication year is retained for citation; first-online dates and conflicting metadata are recorded separately when known. Reprints, new paperback formats, digitization dates, and platform migrations do not create new studies. A DOI's embedded year is not used as a publication date.

English-language search terms and accessible English descriptions dominate retrieval. No claim of multilingual completeness is made. Publisher and journal records or institutional repositories establish bibliographic existence. Individual peer-review status is recorded when explicit; a reputable venue alone is not a completed policy audit.

## Search and selection

Three stages: broad discovery across five overlapping angles (language; digital/computational work; literature and criticism; manuscripts and textual/historical studies; reception and education), targeted gap searches, and a synthesis/contradiction check. Search strings and source URLs are retained with the charted corpus. Search date: October 28, 2026.

Use web search to retrieve primary publisher, journal, institutional, project, and bibliographic-review pages. Follow exact-title and DOI leads. Inspect abstracts and, where accessible and useful, identified sections. Deduplicate by DOI and normalized title, keeping publication/online date variants in one record. Classifications are checked against the inspected source description. Unresolved leads remain outside the main corpus.

This run does not include authenticated searches or exports from MLA International Bibliography, ABELL, International Medieval Bibliography, Scopus, or Web of Science. Web search result rankings and publisher-access constraints limit recall. Three Codex workers cover separate branches; they are not two independent screeners of every record. Selection is breadth-oriented and iterative, without a complete initial discovery register. Consequently total retrieved, excluded, and full-text-screened counts are **not available**; no PRISMA flow totals will be invented. Retained-corpus counts are reproducible from the saved JSON.

## Charting and synthesis

For each retained publication record title, authors, publication year, online year if known, venue, type, DOI/URL, topics, methods, method basis, read scope, focus, and date cautions. Topic and method labels allow multiple assignments. A literary critical framework is distinguished from an analytical procedure: for example, material feminism may frame close reading, while a study of word order may combine corpus retrieval with formal syntax.

Use plain-language categories alongside more specific labels. Abstract-based method classifications remain provisional. Record the author's research aim or argument without converting it into scholarly consensus. Do not assign methodological weaknesses from abstracts or metadata. Do not infer what the user has personally read.

Summarize intersections across topic and method and give contrasting examples. Counts describe the retained corpus only. Do not estimate the proportion of the whole field, rank topics by popularity, infer growth rates, or label a lightly covered area a genuine research gap.

## Reporting and limitations

The synthesis links to the corpus, topic and methodology guides, major source notes, and existing related vault pages. A reporting-status table identifies covered, partial, absent, and inapplicable PRISMA-ScR items. Formal critical appraisal, full-text verification of every argument, comprehensive retraction/COI checks, and librarian/expert validation are not performed. No biomedical evidence hierarchy is imposed on literary or philological work.

AI-assisted web retrieval, charting, and synthesis are disclosed. Confidence values in page metadata are framework bookkeeping judgments, not measured probabilities of correctness.

Backend status: defuddle is installed, but the registry's command syntax required `parse`; the corrected Nature fetch also failed. Browser search/open is the working retrieval route. yt-dlp and perplexity are unavailable; the paid Perplexity key is unset. No paid backend or bibliographic API client was used.


## Incremental amendment — October 28, 2026

The user observed that 64 sources are few compared with their previous manual searches involving hundreds, and requested incremental updates to the same page, synthesizing each new batch into it. B001 is accordingly designated **pilot coverage**, not a completed account of the field. The source register, stable IDs, family definitions, and master synthesis are cumulative. Later batches target wider bibliography- and venue-led retrieval, retain a batch-specific candidate/decision register from the start, and revise earlier synthesis rather than appending isolated essays. The incomplete initial discovery register cannot be reconstructed retrospectively.

Use [[skills/incremental-old-english-literature-review]] and [[references/old-english-review-batch-log]]. Aiming for 50–100 new substantive records per batch is an operational target, not an author-specified quota, a claim that the field contains that many eligible items in a given source, or a stopping rule. Updates run when requested; no recurring automation has been requested or created.
