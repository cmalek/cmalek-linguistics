# Prose Edda

EIEOL lesson 2: Snorri’s *Edda* is a skaldic textbook, not a pagan liturgy. Three parts after the **Prologue**: *Gylfaginning* (myth narratives), *Skáldskaparmál* (diction), *Háttatal* (verse-forms).

## Problem & Motivation

The printed extract is the Prologue’s opening: Creation, Flood, forgotten name of God, then natural philosophy that treats the earth as animate. Krause & Slocum: by Snorri’s day educated Iceland is Christian; older gods are **reinterpreted**, not worshipped in this text.

## Method / Architecture

**Plot of the extract:** Almighty God creates heaven and earth, then Adam and Eve; most people follow worldly desire; Flood except Noah’s ark; eight survivors; again greed; they will not name God and forget the creator. God still gives earthly gifts and wisdom to observe air and earth. Analogies: springs on peaks as blood near head or foot; yearly grass like hair/feathers; topsoil that grows when dug; rocks as teeth/bones. Conclusion: earth is in some sense *kyk* (living), old and mighty; she feeds the living and takes the dead; they name her and trace *ættir* to her.

Classroom ON: *guð* is grammatically **neuter** with masculine concord; middle-voice verbs (*fjǫlgaðisk*, *dreifðisk*); relative *er*.

## Limitations

Do not read this paragraph as “what Vikings believed in 800.” It is 13th-c. euhemerizing cosmology. ^[inferred]
