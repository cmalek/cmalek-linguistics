# Buildings, landscapes, memorials, and reconstruction

The collection juxtaposes churches, ruins, a mound, modern memorials, reconstructed-looking timber buildings, stone carvings, and later statues. It is useful for comparing kinds of representation, but a photograph of an old-looking structure does not establish that the pictured fabric is Anglo-Saxon. ^[inferred]

The Alfred plaque explicitly says it was erected in 1986 and commemorates Alfred’s resettlement of London in 886; it also claims a harbor and market were established at the site by 899. These are statements on a modern commemorative plaque, rather than independently checked ninth-century evidence. A separate memorial visibly bears “CAEDMON.”

Building names, construction phases, locations, and reconstruction dates remain unverified where the image lacks a readable label. ^[ambiguous]
