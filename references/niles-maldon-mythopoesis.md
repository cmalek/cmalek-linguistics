# Niles on Maldon and mythopoesis

John D. Niles, “Maldon and Mythopoesis,” in *Old English Heroic Poems and the Social Life of Texts* (a Yale reprint; DOI [10.12987/9780300129113-024](https://doi.org/10.12987/9780300129113-024)). OpenAlex dates the chapter record 2002. The chapter was not read. This page is the OpenAlex abstract.

Niles treats the defeat at Maldon as a turning point in English fortunes against the Danes, and he tries to reconstruct what Byrhtnoth’s death did to morale and policy in the 990s. His claim about purpose: the poem argues for paying tribute to the Vikings, and it makes that argument by glorifying Byrhtnoth’s death.

That claim sits against other accounts of purpose collected on [[synthesis/Research: The Battle of Maldon]]. Richard Marsden’s *Cambridge Old English Reader* chapter (DOI [10.1017/cbo9780511817069.037](https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511817069.037)) says the defiant speeches are a weak national rallying cry and that the aim may have been local commemoration of Byrhtnoth. [[references/jones-1990-lexical-structure-maldon|Jones]] denies that the poem is an encomium. Niles’s tribute thesis is a distinct reading, known here from the abstract only.
