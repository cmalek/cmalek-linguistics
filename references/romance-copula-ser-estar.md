# Romance Copula: Ser and Estar

The English Wikipedia article [Romance copula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_copula) is a survey, not a corpus study. It is used here for the standard description of Spanish *ser* and *estar* against which the Old English copulas are compared.

## Key claims

- Western Romance builds two copulas from three Latin verbs: *esse* “be,” *stāre* “stand,” and *sedēre* “sit.” *Stāre* becomes a second copula meaning “be temporarily or incidentally.” *Esse* narrows toward “be permanently or essentially.” Spanish, Portuguese, and Catalan carry this furthest.
- Spanish *estar* is *stāre* via *\*estare*. Spanish *ser* mixes *esse* (most of the paradigm) with *sedēre* (the present subjunctive, via Old Spanish *seer*). The infinitive *ser* can come from either *esse* or *sedēre*. The past stem *fui* is from the Indo-European “become” root, the same root as English *be*.
- In the *Cantar de Mio Cid*, *ser* still covers ground that modern Spanish gives to *estar* or *quedar*: *Es pagado* where one might now say *Está satisfecho*.
- The article’s modern rule: *ser* is fundamental nature, identity, essence, or characteristics. *Estar* is the state or condition something happens to be in. It calls this essence versus accident, and it links *estar* etymologically with *state*, *stand*, and *stay*. *Quedar* overlaps with *estar* for a resultant state.

## Limits

The article does not discuss Old English. Essence versus accident is a philosophical gloss of a split that also includes location, the progressive, and resultant participles. Those extra contexts matter for the comparison on [[concepts/ser-estar-and-old-english-be]].
