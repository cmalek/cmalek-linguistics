# Judith (Old English poem)

> [!tldr] Biblical Judith recast as an Anglo-Saxon heroic-religious fragment: God shields the faithful; the surviving text opens at Holofernes’s feast on the fourth day.

## Problem & Motivation

The **Book of Judith** is deuterocanonical: in the Septuagint and Catholic/Orthodox Old Testaments; out of the Hebrew canon; Protestant apocrypha. Hebrew name *Yəhūdīt* “praised / Jewess.” A Jewish widow uses beauty and charm to kill an Assyrian general besieging Bethulia and so save Jerusalem. Most modern scholars treat it as parable or theological fiction (anachronisms); Catholic tradition long set it in Manasseh’s reign. Original language is disputed (Hebrew/Aramaic vs Greek modeled on Hebrew). ^[ambiguous]

**Holofernes:** Assyrian general in that book; Greek Ὀλοφέρνης. Etymology from Old Persian *\*Varufarnāh* “with wide-reaching glory.” Dispatched by “Nebuchadnezzar” after Judah withheld aid; bans other gods; ignores Achior; sieges Bethulia and cuts the water. Judith enters the camp, gets him drunk, beheads him. Traditional Catholic identification with Ashurbanipal’s *turtanu*; medieval Hebrew versions recode him as Nicanor / the Greek as a stand-in for Seleucid antagonists. Later literature (Chaucer, Dante) uses him as pride cast down.

The OE poem recasts this in heroic-religious idiom. Theme in the Aerobics headnote: God rewards those who believe and trust him with victory, glory, and wealth.

## Method / Architecture

Lost beginning presumably covered Holofernes’s siege of Bethulia, the leaders’ readiness to surrender, and Judith’s three-day feigned defection with one maid. The fragment begins with her finding *mundbyrd* from God at greatest need, then Holofernes’s wine-feast for the senior thegns (*þȳ fēorðan dōgore* after she first sought him). He drinks the *duguð* into a death-like stupor, has the *ēadigan mæġð* fetched to a gold-netted bed-tent he can see out of but others cannot see into, intending *wīdle ond womme*; the Lord will not allow it.

Shares the Nowell Codex with [[references/beowulf]] (binding order of *Judith* and *Beowulf* has shifted historically). Malek’s facing PDF (Aerobics text) flags **hypermetric** lines in the feast and beheading stretch — extra lifts in the verse, not a second poem. Classroom notes track variation (*metod*, *dryhten*) rather than collapsing divine names.

Other Insular treatments: Aldhelm (Latin); Ælfric (OE homiletic). Griffith (Exeter, 1997) is the commentary edition named in the source.

## Limitations

Beginning of the poem is missing; do not reconstruct the siege from this fragment alone. Read the biblical book alongside.
