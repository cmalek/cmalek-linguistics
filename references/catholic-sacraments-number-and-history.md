# Number and history of the Catholic sacraments

Wikipedia, “Sacraments of the Catholic Church.” [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacraments_of_the_Catholic_Church).

## Key claims

- The catechism’s seven are baptism, confirmation, eucharist, penance, anointing of the sick, holy orders, and matrimony.
- The early number was variable. Peter Damian listed eleven, including the ordination of kings. Hugh of St Victor enumerated nearly thirty, with baptism and communion set apart.
- Peter Lombard’s *Sentences* set out the seven now taught. The Council of Florence (1439) listed those seven. The Council of Trent, session 7, anathematized anyone who says there are more or fewer than seven: baptism, confirmation, eucharist, penance, extreme unction, order, and matrimony.

## Limits

The same article says the Fourth Lateran Council of 1215 “confirmed” Lombard’s seven. The English text of the 1215 canons, read for [[references/fourth-lateran-council-1215]], confesses the eucharist, baptism, and penitence and does not enumerate seven rites. That sentence of the survey is ^[ambiguous] against the canon text. Florence and Trent do make the list explicit.
