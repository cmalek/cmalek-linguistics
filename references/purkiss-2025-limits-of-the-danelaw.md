# The Limits of the Danelaw (Purkiss 2025)

Richard Purkiss, “The Limits of the Danelaw,” *English Historical Review* 140, no. 607 (2025): 1250–1289. DOI [10.1093/ehr/ceaf048](https://doi.org/10.1093/ehr/ceaf048).

The treaty of Alfred and Guthrum, from the 880s, runs up the Thames to the Lea, up the Lea to its source, in a straight line to Bedford, and up the Great Ouse as far as Watling Street, which crosses the river at Stony Stratford. Purkiss says later historians often continue that frontier indefinitely along Watling Street. That continuation is possible and unproved. Watling Street is not named as the Danelaw boundary in contemporary sources. The road from London toward Wroxeter is a plausible line of maximum Scandinavian political control, and it is an interpolation when it is drawn as the legal border.

He also argues that *Dena lagu* means the law of the people called Danes, and that the region those texts assign to them lies between the Welland and the Tees, not a Thames-to-Tees province of “Danish custom.” The place-name map in [[references/fellows-jensen-1989-scandinavian-settlement]] is a different kind of evidence: settlement names, not the legal term.
