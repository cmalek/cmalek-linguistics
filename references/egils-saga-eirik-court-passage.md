# Egils saga: Egill before Eiríkr at York

Lesson 5 of the UT Old Norse Online course ([[skills/ut-eieol-old-norse]]) pairs a short saga excerpt with a cultural essay on Scandinavian merchant trade and with grammar sections on numerals, strong verbs I-II and adverbs.

## The passage

**Setting.** Egill ([[entities/egill-skallagrimsson]]), wrecked in Northumbria, learns that his enemy Eiríkr Blóðøx is king at York. His friend Arinbjǫrn, a retainer of Eiríkr, brings him to court and argues for delay. Queen Gunnhildr and the king's men debate killing him. The excerpt begins at the end of that debate.

**Story beats.**
1. Arinbjǫrn: if Egill is to get no settlement, then *drengskapr* (nobility) demands a week's respite and leave to depart, since Egill came of his own will and expected peace.
2. Gunnhildr: Arinbjǫrn is more loyal to Egill than to the king; in a week Egill could reach King Aðalsteinn (Athelstan) of England.
3. Arinbjǫrn threatens to fight beside Egill with his followers; the king would "pay dearly" for Egill's life.
4. Eiríkr yields to the extent of hearing him out; Egill steps forward, demands silence and recites his poem.
5. The four stanzas quoted are the start of the praise poem: Egill sailed west, brings "Odin's mead" (poetry) to England, and begins to praise the king's battles. The lesson notes that the poem wins him his life.

## Language points

- **Dual pronouns in address.** *þit* addresses king and queen together ("you two"); *vit Egill* means "I and Egill", the dual with the second member named. See dual forms in [[concepts/old-norse-grammar]].
- *þér* "you (pl.)" descends from earlier *ér* with *þ* borrowed from the verb ending; *muntu* = *munt þú*, with enclitic pronoun.
- **Subjunctives** in purpose and expectation clauses: *at hann forði sér*, *vænti sér at því friðar*.
- **Negatives** with *engi* (*enga sætt* "no settlement"): irregular paradigm covered in [[concepts/old-norse-numerals-and-strong-verbs]].
- *vara* is impersonal with genitive: *mik varði* "I expected".
- *frá* in *Flestr maðr of frá* is the past of *fregna* "hear of" (cf. Old English *fregn*), not the preposition.
- **Poetic diction:** *of* is a metrically useful particle often used adverbially; *Óðins mjǫð* "Odin's mead" is a kenning for poetry. Compare the skaldic tradition described in [[references/prose-edda]].

## Cultural essay (same lesson)

Scandinavian culture depended on merchant trade as well as raiding: furs for honey, wheat, wine and cloth in England; the market at Kaupang (Oslo Fjord, defensible site, tents on turf walls) versus year-round Hedeby (walled, c. 60 acres, craftsmen, Frisian and Saxon graves) and Birka (Swedish hub from c. 870 for Dnieper and Volga routes). A slave trade fed Arab markets; the eastern routes brought tens of thousands of Arab silver coins to Sweden and gave the Rus their name. Context: [[concepts/viking-age-expansion]], [[concepts/norse-material-culture-and-society]].

## Authorship note

Author unknown; some suspect Snorri Sturluson ([[entities/snorri-sturluson]]), who lived at Egill's old farm. Main manuscript 14th c.; a paper fragment from the mid-13th c.
