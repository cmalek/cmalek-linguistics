# Vezzosi 2003: Where himself comes from

Letizia Vezzosi, “Where himself comes from” (2003), retrieved from DOI [10.6092/lef_17_p205](https://doi.org/10.6092/lef_17_p205). The repository text was read. The argument is grammaticalization, illustrated from a corpus that includes Ælfric and Wulfstan’s homilies.

Old English has no dedicated reflexive anaphor. The personal pronoun marks both coreference and disjoint reference. *self* is an intensifier, adnominal or adverbial, and it is added when coreference would otherwise be unclear. ^[extracted]

She places the rise of the dedicated pronoun + *self* reflexive in Middle English, under the pressure on written English after the Conquest, and she says the Modern English pattern is already in place in Early Modern English. Frisian, on her comparison, still looks like Old English. ^[extracted]

The opened text does not give a rate of *sylf* in Wulfstan against earlier prose. Wulfstan is in the corpus. The period claim is Middle English, not a change inside late Old English.
