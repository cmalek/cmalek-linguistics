# Fernández Cuesta and Langmuir 2025: Northumbrian subjunctive

Julia María Fernández Cuesta and Christopher Langmuir, “Mood Selection in the Old Northumbrian Gloss to Durham MS A.iv.19,” *Transactions of the Philological Society* (2025). The open PDF from idUS was read.

Their [[concepts/old-english-dialects|West Saxon]] present subjunctive table is cited from Hogg and Fulk 2011: 214. Strong verbs: *drife*, *drifen*. Weak class I: *lǣre*, *lǣren*. Weak class II: *lufige*, *lufigen*. ^[extracted]

They say late Northumbrian lost unstressed *-n* early, so the subjunctive merges in form with the infinitive. The Northumbrian present subjunctive cell they print, from Ross and Berndt, is a set of spellings rather than one ending: for class II, *lufiga*, *lufage*, *lufie*, *lufaga*, *lufaige*, *lufega*, *lufege*, and related forms. ^[extracted]

The article’s own topic is which mood the Durham gloss chooses. The paradigm tables are the part used here. Hogg and Fulk 2011 was not opened.
