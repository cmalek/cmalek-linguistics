# Nevalainen 2017: Early Modern English

Terttu Nevalainen, “Early Modern English,” *Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Linguistics*, published 22 August 2017. The opening sections on periodization were read. The rest of the chapter was not used for vowel-shift claims.

Nevalainen says linguistic periodization is arbitrary because change is continuous. Early Modern English is commonly treated as 1500–1700, and that is the window of her chapter. She records other cuts. The six-part *Cambridge History of the English Language* starts the period at 1476, when printing reached England, and ends it at 1776 (Lass 1999, as she cites it). Görlach (1991) uses 1500 and 1700 for spelling homogenization and for the settling of grammatical categories. She notes that those linguistic features have no exact start or end.

The abstract lists the Great Vowel Shift among gradual pronunciation developments of these two centuries, beside standardization of spelling and changes in grammar and word order. The chapter as read here does not reconstruct the vowel paths.
