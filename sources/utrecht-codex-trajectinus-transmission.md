# Utrecht: transmission of the [[references/prose-edda|Prose Edda]]

## Findings

Utrecht dates its paper Codex Trajectinus to the 1590s and describes it as a copy of a lost medieval exemplar. R and T share errors, indicating a common ancestor. U carries the explicit Snorri attribution and a shorter text; the principal witnesses differ in contents and losses.

## Limits

A late copy can transmit earlier readings. Shared readings can also be inherited errors rather than independent corroboration. ^[inferred] The library’s account of relationships does not supply an autograph or establish that every passage belongs to one authorial version. It cannot date the speech represented by a normalized classroom text.
