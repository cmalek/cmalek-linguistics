# Walters 2004: [[concepts/old-english|Old English]] case syncretism

Denise E. Walters, “Loss and Consequence: An Examination of the Old English Case Marking System As Opposed to that of Other Old Germanic Languages,” *Colorado Research in Linguistics* 17, no. 1 (2004). The article was read. It is a course paper. The chronology below is her report of Campbell and Moore, not a new count.

She reports Campbell 1959: 369 for early confusion of unstressed back vowels, visible in ninth-century [[concepts/old-english-dialects|Kentish]] charters, and Campbell 1959: 379 for eleventh-century coalescence of front and back suffix vowels. ^[extracted]

She reports that final /m/ becomes /n/, complete by the end of the eleventh century on Moore 1928, and that Campbell 1959: 378 already finds dative plural *-un* beside *-um* in early West Saxon by the beginning of that century. With the vowel reduced, *-um* falls together with *-an*. ^[extracted]

Informs [[concepts/old-english-early-late-writing]] and [[synthesis/Research: Early and Late Old English Spelling and Inflection]]. Campbell 1959 was not opened.
