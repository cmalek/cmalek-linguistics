# Condorelli 2019: West Saxon ie and y

Marco Condorelli, “Irregularity of the ‘ie’ spellings in West Saxon English: Remarks on variation in third-person pronouns,” *SELIM* 24 (2019): 29–52. DOI [10.17811/selim.24.2019.29-52](https://doi.org/10.17811/selim.24.2019.29-52). The article text was read.

Late West Saxon writing begins in the second half of the tenth century. Condorelli prefers “regularisation” to “standardisation,” and he says early and late West Saxon are probably not lineally descended, citing Hoad 2014. ^[extracted]

The correspondence he treats as general, citing Campbell 1959 §§302 and Hogg 1992 §5.167: early West Saxon ⟨ie⟩ matches late West Saxon ⟨y⟩, and before palatals the late graph is ⟨i⟩. His comparison of Chronicle manuscripts A–D finds that pattern, and also finds that the rise of ⟨y⟩ is not a simple mirror of the fall of ⟨ie⟩. ^[extracted]

He separates the spelling fact from the sound. ⟨y⟩ can mark rounding or laxness, and the grammars do not agree which. ^[extracted]

Informs [[concepts/old-english-early-late-writing]] and [[synthesis/Research: Early and Late Old English Spelling and Inflection]]. The Campbell and Hogg sections he cites were not opened for this review.
