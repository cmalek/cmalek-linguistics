# Kay and Alexander 2015: Diachronic and Synchronic Thesauruses

Christian Kay and Marc Alexander, “Diachronic and Synchronic Thesauruses,” in *The Oxford Handbook of Lexicography*, ed. Philip Durkin (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2015). The Glasgow author manuscript was read for the sections on historical thesauruses, [[concepts/old-english|Old English]], and glossaries. The chapter’s survey of modern learner thesauruses was not used.

They warn that the size of a thesaurus category can look like evidence of what a society cared about, and that this reading has to be checked against survival. The [[entities/thesaurus-of-old-english]] is lexically rich in weapons and warriors, “showing their role in the literature of the time, or at least in what has come down to us.” Because that thesaurus rests on a complete, fixed corpus, a thin category is evidence about the corpus. Their example is terms of endearment between two people: the paucity “is not a reflection on the Anglo-Saxon peoples, but rather one on what writings we have of theirs that survive.”

Semantic ordering can also join a rare Old English word to a much later one. They cite OE *becca* “a pick or mattock,” linked to the same meaning of *beck*, which the *OED* does not record again until 1875.

On glossaries, they describe Latin texts with Old English glosses from the eighth century onward. Wordlists were gathered for teaching Latin. Favourite topics were the body and its parts, precious stones, medicinal herbs, and natural kinds: animals, birds, fish, and plants. The practice continues into Middle English (1150–1500).
