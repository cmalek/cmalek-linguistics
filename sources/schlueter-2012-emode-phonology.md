# Schlüter 2012: Early Modern English phonology

Julia Schlüter, “Early Modern English: Phonology,” in Alexander Bergs and Laurel Brinton (eds.), *Historical Linguistics of English* (HSK 34.1), de Gruyter, 2012, pp. 589–604. The text used here is the author’s offprint PDF. She points the Great Vowel Shift itself to Manfred Krug, chapter 48 of the same handbook, and then still gives the traditional statement because later vowel changes depend on it.

What she states:

- The shift raised Middle English /aː/, /ɛː/, /eː/, /ɔː/, and /oː/ by one step, and diphthongized /iː/ and /uː/, which were already as close as a vowel can be.
- The changes are assumed to be causally connected, so the shift is presented as a chain. The precise mechanics remain debated.
- Initial signs go back at least to the fifteenth century. The first stage was completed in the sixteenth century. Her figure for the system is set around 1600.
- The chain itself did not merge vowel phonemes. Around 1700, /iː/ from Middle English /eː/ and /eː/ from Middle English /ɛː/ fell together, with variation still in the eighteenth century. That is the loss of the *see*/*sea* and *meet*/*meat* contrast.
- The reflex of Middle English /aː/, as in *make* and *ale*, reached /eː/ in the seventeenth century and diphthongized to /eɪ/ around 1800. A few words from the *meat* class, notably *great* and *break*, joined that lower vowel instead of /iː/.
- Middle English /ɔː/, as in *boat*, had moved to /oː/. From the eighteenth century the diphthong /oʊ/ prevailed in the standard, and became Received Pronunciation /əʊ/ around 1920.
- The quality of the new diphthongs from /iː/ and /uː/ is controversial. A centered /əɪ/ and /əʊ/ is the older reconstruction. Schlüter reports that Faiß (1989) and Lass (1999) reconstruct /ɛɪ/ and /ɔʊ/. In standard British English the modern /aɪ/ and /aʊ/ were reached in the eighteenth century.

**Limits.** This is a handbook synthesis, and it sets the chain aside for Krug’s chapter. That chapter was not read. Lass 1999 was not read; the diphthong-quality point is Schlüter’s report.
