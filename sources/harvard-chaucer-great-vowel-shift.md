# Harvard Chaucer page: The Great Vowel Shift

“The Great Vowel Shift,” Harvard’s Geoffrey Chaucer Website, [chaucer.fas.harvard.edu/pages/great-vowel-shift](https://chaucer.fas.harvard.edu/pages/great-vowel-shift). Read from the search extract of that page. The registered `defuddle` command failed, so this note does not rest on a second HTML parse.

The page says the long stressed vowels changed places of articulation beginning in the twelfth century and continuing until the eighteenth, with the main effects in the fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries. It says the change affects only long, stressed vowels. The stressed vowel of Middle English *my* shifted; the unstressed vowel of *only* did not. It uses John Hart’s *Orthographie* (1569), and his proposed phonetic alphabet, as evidence for sixteenth-century pronunciation, and it ties the mismatch to later spelling-reform attempts.

**Limits.** This is a Chaucer course page. Its twelfth-century start is broader than the conventional “around 1400” dating in [[sources/stenbrenden-2016-long-vowel-shifts]]. The vowel table in the extract is simplified, and one back-vowel cell is not reliable enough to copy. Use the page for the teaching chronology and the stressed-long-vowel restriction. Use the handbook and monograph pages for the phonetic path.
