# Owen-Crocker 1979: Wynflæd's Wardrobe

Gale R. Owen-Crocker, “Wynflæd’s Wardrobe,” *Anglo-Saxon England* 8 (1979): 195–222. The journal abstract was read. The article and her 1976 thesis were not read page by page. The thesis abstract, “Anglo-Saxon Costume” (Newcastle, 1976), was read for how she divides the evidence by period.

The 1979 abstract says Anglo-Saxon wills rarely itemize clothing, and descriptive references to non-military clothing are uncommon in Old English texts generally. Many garment-names are documented. Some occur only in glossaries or in translations from Latin and may never have been in common use in England. Some words are of uncertain meaning. For most names, the sex of the wearer and the relative value of the garment are unknown. Wynflæd’s mid-tenth-century will is unusual because its garment-names were certainly worn by a woman at a known date and were valuable enough to bequeath.

The thesis abstract organizes the evidence by what each century actually preserves. Pagan-period dress is reconstructed from cemetery archaeology. Early Christian dress comes mainly from carving, sculpture, and Latin writings. The vocabulary of clothing terms is compiled from material written down mainly in the last two centuries of the Anglo-Saxon period, though some words may be much older. Terms are grouped by function: materials, outer garments, body garments, loin and leg coverings, footwear, headgear, accessories, general terms, and clasps.
