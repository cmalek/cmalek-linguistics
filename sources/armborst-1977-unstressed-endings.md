# Armborst 1977: Unstressed endings in [[references/beowulf|Beowulf]]

David Armborst, “Evidence for Phonetic Weakening in Inflectional Syllables in Beowulf,” *Leeds Studies in English*, n.s. 9 (1977): 1–18. The PDF was read.

Occasional spellings can show a reduced vowel, a copying error, or an analogical shift. Armborst’s point is that those have to be separated. ^[extracted]

For the preterite plural subjunctive, *-en* written *-on* or *-an* is early and common in West Saxon. He follows Marckwardt in taking that as leveling from the indicative plural *-on*, because the singular indicative and subjunctive already shared *-e*. He cites Campbell §735(e) and Brunner for *-an* as a variant of that leveled *-on*, and Campbell §377 for a possible [[concepts/old-english-dialects|Kentish]] origin of the *-an* spelling. He argues against reading every *-an* as a reverse spelling of *-en*. ^[extracted]

Marckwardt’s infinitive counts, as Armborst reports them: 35 forms in *-en* against 1,458 in *-an* in fifteen texts, and only the latest text reaches about 10 percent leveled infinitives. ^[extracted]

Reed’s non-northern figures, as Armborst reports them, keep final *-n* in the present plural subjunctive at about 93 percent early in the eleventh century and about 85 percent late in that century. Loss of *-n* in the preterite plural is later than in the present plural. ^[extracted]
