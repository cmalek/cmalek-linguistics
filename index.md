## Synthesis

- [[synthesis/alfred-the-great-x-germanic-sound-change]] — The textbook vowel is the West Saxon copy dialect. Published shares disagree: about 60 percent, or up to 90 percent written in West Saxon. ( #old-english #phonology #history)
- [[synthesis/bede-x-christianity-in-anglo-saxon-england]] — Bede's Historia is a post-Whitby computist's plot of conversion, and the narrative path by which Cædmon's hymn enters the wiki. ( #old-english #history #religion)
- [[synthesis/danelaw-x-old-english-dialects]] — Anglian vowel cues and Norse loans share the north-eastern map and record two histories: inherited sound change, then contact. ( #old-english #dialects #norse #history)
- [[synthesis/danelaw-x-viking-age-expansion]] — Norse loans, place-names, and slow pronoun spread measure bilingual settlement, beside a short-lived 878 treaty and a later tribute war. ( #old-english #old-norse #history #settlement)
- [[synthesis/germanic-sound-change-x-old-english-grammar]] — Strong-verb vowel rows are ablaut; later i-mutation and breaking add alternations; analogy is why most verbs show a dental past. ( #old-english #phonology #grammar)
- [[synthesis/ocr-character-inventory-x-old-english-pronunciation]] — The marks an Old English textbook adds for pronunciation (macrons, dotted ċ ġ) are the rarest characters an OCR model must emit and the ones a global error rate hides. ( #ocr #old-english #phonology #unicode)
- [[synthesis/oe-prefixes-for-fore-oth-x-bosworth-toller]] — The flagged clash over for-/fore- length marks also matters downstream: a generator that takes Bosworth-Toller as its lemma list inherits editorial splits as separate lemmas. ( #old-english #lexicography #prefixes #tooling)
- [[synthesis/old-english-g-palatalization-x-danelaw]] — A velar g where Old English had a palatal one is a weak marker of Norse contact, like the sk test, but weaker Northumbrian palatalization is itself explained by contact, which risks circularity. ( #old-english #phonology #norse #history)
- [[synthesis/old-english-lemmatisation-x-old-english-inflectional-class]] — Neural lemmatiser errors cluster on strong-verb ablaut, the one field its output never states: inflectional class. Class is the missing variable, and compounds show it most. ( #old-english #lemmatisation #morphology #nlp)
- [[synthesis/Research: Beon, Wesan, and Weorthan]] — Three-round survey: is is the particular present, beon the generic or future, weorþan the transition. Ser and estar do not match those poles. ( #old-english #copula #verbs #spanish #research)
- [[synthesis/Research: Future Tense in Old English]] — Three-round survey: Old English has no future inflection. The present is the usual future; willan, sculan, and beon are partial, debated means. ( #old-english #tense #grammar #grammaticalization #research)
- [[synthesis/Research: Old English G-Palatalization]] — How OE g became palatal. Hogg 1992 §§7.15-17 now supplies the rule, the unstressed-e cases, and his Norse list. Hogg 1979 and Campbell §§426-430 remain unread. ( #old-english #phonology #palatalization #historical-linguistics #research)
- [[synthesis/Research: Old English Morphology Generation]] — Three-round survey: class lives in Nerthus and Tichý, government in PaVeDa’s 80 verbs, and no generator labels full dialect paradigms. ( #old-english #morphology #lexicography #dialects #research)
