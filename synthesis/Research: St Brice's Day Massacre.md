# Research: St Brice's Day Massacre

## Overview

On 13 November 1002 [[entities/aethelred-the-unready]] ordered Danes in England killed. The evidence is two contemporary texts plus disputed graves. The same years frame [[entities/wulfstan-of-york]]'s rise (archbishop in 1002) and the road to [[entities/cnut-the-great]]'s throne. Documented: chronology. Argued: causation.

## Key Findings

- ASC says a plot to kill the king and council; the 1004 Frideswide charter boasts of a "most just extermination." [[references/postgrad-chronicles-st-brices-day]]
- Scope was probably frontier towns, mercenaries, and recent settlers, not the Danelaw. [[references/wikipedia-st-brices-day-massacre]]
- Archaeology fits but does not prove: Oxford and Ridgeway Hill. [[references/archaeology-magazine-2013-vengeance-on-the-vikings]]
- **Swein:** returned from 1003; revenge for Gunnhild rests on Malmesbury alone. [[entities/swein-forkbeard]]
- **Æthelred:** the massacre is one episode in a reign of tribute (1002, 1007, 1012), Thorkell, flight in 1013, and return in 1014. [[references/worldhistory-danish-conquest-of-england]]
- **Wulfstan:** the date is secure. He became archbishop of York and bishop of Worcester in 1002. No contemporary text read here names him as the author of the killing. [[references/wilcox-2000-st-brice-wulfstan|Wilcox 2000]] is the essay written to the question, and it is still unread. [[references/savill-2021-st-brictius|Savill]] bundles that essay with Roach, *Æthelred*, pp. 186–251, as the case that reformist prelates, Wulfstan among them, planned the purge. A Scholar snippet of Schwartz 2018 has Wilcox leaving any role in the execution uncertain ("if he had one at all"). [[references/roach-2016-aethelred-massacre|Roach's own 2016 essay]] explains the massacre as a strike against recent mercenaries and does not name Wulfstan. The biography pages Savill cites were not opened. ^[ambiguous] His other connections stay as before: the *Sermo Lupi* of 1014, Æthelred's 1014 code, then Cnut's laws. [[references/roep-titterington-sermo-lupi]]
- **Cnut:** after Swein's death in 1014 and Assandun on 18 October 1016 he became king; Wulfstan stayed to draft for him. Roach and MacDonald (via Wikipedia) see the suspicion bred by the purges as helping that outcome. [[concepts/danish-conquest-of-england]]

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- **St John's radiocarbon:** postgrad blog says before 1002; Wikipedia and Archaeology Magazine give 960–1020, overlapping 1002. Unresolved.
- **Ridgeway count:** 51 vs 54 beheaded.
- **Charter date:** 1004 (usual) vs 1002 (one site).
- **Revenge motive:** Stenton-style provocation vs power bid; Gunnhild unsupported.
- **Wulfstan and 1002:** still not a documented order in his name. Wilcox 2000 and Roach pp. 186–251 remain unread. The open witnesses disagree in emphasis: Savill cites both for a planner's role; Schwartz's snippet has Wilcox unsure of any part in the execution; Roach's Yale essay omits him. Whether the *Sermo* alludes to the massacre is still unchecked.
- **Savill 2021** is now read. The liturgical argument (Brice's day chosen over Martinmas) is his hypothesis. See [[references/savill-2021-st-brictius]].
- Roach's biography as a book is still unread. His 11 November 2016 essay is filed.
- Alney partition not confirmed in a fetched page.
