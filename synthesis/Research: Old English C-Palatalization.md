# Research: [[concepts/old-english|Old English]] C-Palatalization

Research checked on 20 October 2026. Here **stop list** means exclusions from automatic palatal marking, not common function words. The subject is single and doubled **c**, including **nc**; **sc**, **cg**, and **cw** need separate treatment.

Old English spelling alone cannot reliably determine whether c is velar or palatal. A useful reference must distinguish an inherited sound change, its later affricated pronunciation, and a modern editor's dot. The list below is a documented working inventory, **not a claim to enumerate every Old English lexeme, compound, manuscript spelling, or inflected form**. No exhaustive exception lexicon was located. ^[inferred]

## What the current scholarship changes

The latest directly relevant reconstruction located is Arjen Versloot's article, online July 2025 and printed in March 2026. It questions the traditional vowel chronology and expressly leaves some palatalization questions unresolved; it does not supply a replacement c-exception dictionary. [[sources/versloot-2026-reconstructing-old-english]]

The distinction between **palatalization** and **assibilation** matters. A consonant can have fronted articulation without becoming [tʃ]. The convenient reading convention ċ = [tʃ] should not be imposed on every early date or dialect. [[sources/laker-2007-palatalization]]; [[sources/lass-laing-2013-ik]]

For the north, Laker 2021 and Versloot 2023 question the automatic explanation that /k/ must have replaced native /tʃ/ through Norse contact. Native northern developments and southern lexical diffusion are alternatives. Laker 2021 was available here only through its abstract and Versloot's discussion. [[sources/laker-2021-place-names]]; [[sources/versloot-2023-yorkshire]]

## Working rules

These are rules about **historical sounds**, not adjacent modern letters. They summarize Wright §§309–311 alongside the Hogg distinctions already documented in the vault. They are a practical default for conventional southern/[[concepts/old-english-dialects|West Saxon]] reading, not a complete reconstruction of every dialect. [[sources/wright-1914-c-palatalization]]; [[references/hogg-1992-palatalization]]

1. **Initially**, c before an inherited front vowel generally becomes palatal. A diphthong can trigger this through its front first element: ċeaf, ċēap, ċēosan. A vowel newly fronted from a back vowel does not automatically do so: cynn, cēpan.
2. **Medially**, earlier following *i or *j is a central trigger for c/cc/nc: bryċe, sēċan, þeċċan, þenċan. The conditioning sound may disappear before the recorded form. Following e or æ alone is not a universal medial trigger: æcer, sprecan.
3. **After i/ī**, final c is normally palatal in the conventional southern reconstruction: iċ, dīċ, līċ, piċ. Do not extend that to every preceding front vowel: bæc and blæc retain /k/. A following back vowel can preserve the velar in related forms.
4. **Class II -ian does not establish historical adjacency to *i or *j.** Macian, lōcian, prician, liccian, and þancian are velar examples. Consult the history of the ending rather than reading its present i as a trigger.
5. **Respect paradigm alternations and analogy.** A lemma-wide replacement cannot safely represent every form. Wright contrasts ċēosan with curon/coren and brecan with briċþ. Hogg's account distinguishes dīċ from dicas and sēċan from sēcþ.
6. **Inspect each c separately.** In cyċene “kitchen”, initial c is velar and medial ċ palatal; in cryċċ “crutch”, initial cr- is velar and final ċċ palatal. The sequences sc and cg are not independent ordinary c tokens.

The word **front** includes y phonetically, but **not every y has the same history**. “Never palatalize before y” is as unsafe as “always palatalize before y”. Compare cynn and cyssan with ċȳse and ċyriċe. [[sources/freeborn-old-english-pronunciation]]

## Exclusion list: looks palatal, retains velar c

The following are exclusions from a broad spelling heuristic. Most are regular historical outcomes, not exceptions to a correctly specified sound law. Labels refer to the c under discussion, not every consonant in the word.

### Initial c before a front-looking vowel

Wright §310 explicitly gives all the following except cǣġ, which Freeborn supplies. The historical grouping follows Wright §309; it is not a new etymological analysis of each lexeme.

| Form | Meaning | Why a spelling rule misfires |
|---|---|---|
| cēlan | cool, make cool | Secondary front vowel; Wright derives it from *kōljan. |
| cemban | comb | Secondary front vowel; from *kambjan. |
| cēne | bold, keen | Front-looking ē, but velar c. |
| cennan | beget, give birth, produce | Front-looking e, but velar c. |
| cēpan | keep, heed | Front-looking ē, but velar c. |
| cynn | kin, race, kind | y does not prove an old palatalizing environment. |
| cyning | king | The same warning, including later ci- spellings. |
| cyssan | kiss, verb | Velar before y. |
| cȳþan | make known | Velar before ȳ. |
| cempa | warrior | Velar c in a loan-derived formation; not ċempa. |
| cyċene | kitchen | Initial c only is excluded; do not suppress the medial dot. |
| cǣġ, also cæġ/cēġ-type spellings | key | Initial c is velar; the final ġ is a separate question. |

Sources: [[sources/wright-1914-c-palatalization|Wright §§309–310]]; [[sources/freeborn-old-english-pronunciation|Freeborn p. 14]]. Compounds and derivatives need their own occurrence-level check. ^[inferred]

### Medial or final c next to a front vowel

| Form | Meaning | What must be excluded |
|---|---|---|
| æcer | field, acre | Medial c before e and after æ remains velar. |
| bæc | back | Final c after æ is velar. |
| blæc | black | Same warning. |
| brecan | break | Medial c after e is velar in this infinitive. |
| sprecan, later specan | speak | Neither preceding e nor a later reduced spelling makes c palatal. |
| ficol | deceitful, cunning | c after i is velar in Wright's inventory. |
| hnecca | neck | cc after e remains velar. |
| sticca | stick | cc after i remains velar. |
| liccian | lick | Recorded -cci- does not establish a palatal outcome. |
| lōcian | look | The written -ci- sequence is velar. |
| macian | make | The written -ci- sequence is velar; Wright gives *makōjan. |
| prician | prick | The written -ci- sequence is velar. |
| þancian | thank | nc remains velar; compare palatal þenċan below. |
| drincan | drink | Preceding i does not override the following back vowel. |

Source: [[sources/wright-1914-c-palatalization|Wright §§309–310]]. These are attested examples from a grammar, not a generated list of all inflections.

### Forms and candidates that need separate records

- **dicas**, “ditches”, and **ġelīcung**, “likeness”: velar forms in Hogg's rule summary, despite the preceding ī. Do not automatically propagate the dot from dīċ or from another member of the family. [[references/hogg-1992-palatalization]]
- **sēcþ**, “seeks”: the velar beside sēċan belongs in a form-specific record. Modern seek does not establish that the OE infinitive had /k/. [[references/hogg-1992-palatalization]]
- **strīcan**, “stroke, move along”: Freeborn lists velar c despite ī. [[sources/freeborn-old-english-pronunciation]]
- **Kent/Cent, recenian, þicce, frec**, and the complete **-lic/-līce** families: retain as audit candidates rather than claiming a completed scholarly exception entry here. Frec is a velar example in the existing Hogg note; the others need direct entry/edition checks. No universal suffix override is justified by this research. ^[ambiguous]

## Reverse list: palatal despite a misleading surface form

### Hidden historical trigger before an apparent back vowel

Each word below appears in Wright's palatal inventory (§311). The exact dots are modern normalization; Wright's original list does not use this dot convention. Do not pronounce all these with /k/ simply because a follows the c.

| Form | Meaning | Relevant c |
|---|---|---|
| sēċan, sēċean | seek | Medial ċ; Modern English seek is misleading. |
| þenċan, þenċean | think | nċ; Modern English think is misleading. |
| þynċan | seem | nċ before the written a. |
| drenċan | submerge, drench | nċ before a. |
| streċċan | stretch | ċċ before a. |
| weċċan | awaken, arouse | ċċ before a. |
| tǣċan | teach | ċ before a. |
| þeċċan | cover | ċċ before a; explicitly exemplified in §309. |
| feċċan | fetch | Palatal ċċ; its etymology is discussed separately in §309's note. |
| wiċċe | witch | Palatal ċċ; doubling is not a reason to force /k/. |

Source: [[sources/wright-1914-c-palatalization|Wright §§309–311]]. This set contrasts particularly well with macian/lōcian and þancian.

### Other misleading cues

| Forms | Meaning | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| ċīese / ċȳse | cheese | y need not mean retained /k/. |
| ċiriċe / ċyriċe | church | Both c's are palatal in the conventional southern reading. |
| cryċċ | crutch | Initial cr- is /kr/; the final ċċ is palatal. |
| cyċene | kitchen | Initial c is /k/; the medial ċ is palatal. |
| myċel | great, much | Medial ċ; apparent y does not block it. |
| bēċ | books | Palatal c although the singular bōc is velar. |
| lǣċe | physician, leech | Palatal medial c, not inferred merely from following e. |
| smiēċ | smoke | Palatal in Wright's inventory; the back-looking diphthong spelling misleads. |
| benċ, stenċ | bench; smell, odour | A consonant before c is not a universal blocker. |
| hwilċ/hwelċ, swilċ/swelċ | which; such | Earlier -līk explains why a present lc cluster can be palatal. |
| ċeald, ċealf, ċeorfan | cold; calf; carve | The southern OE forms are palatal despite modern /k/. |

Sources: [[sources/wright-1914-c-palatalization|Wright §311 and note 2]]; [[sources/freeborn-old-english-pronunciation|Freeborn pp. 13–14]]. Wright's inventory is historical evidence; it does not prove a uniform [tʃ] realization in every OE dialect.

## Complete bounded inventories from Wright

For reproducibility, these are the **full example inventories in §§310–311 of the 1914 second edition**, not a full OE dictionary. Macrons and dots are normalized cautiously; compounds, variant spellings, and inflected forms elsewhere in the grammar are not exhaustively collected. The OCR was read with obvious character errors corrected; the scanned pages were not visually collated.

**§310, velar c:** cēlan, cemban, corn, cūþ, cynn, cnēo; camb (also comb), cēne, cennan, cēpan, cōl, coss, cū, cuman, cyning, cyssan, cȳþan, clǣne, climban, cnotta, cræft, cwēn; candel, copor, cycene (initial c), camp, cempa, cōc, cuppe; æcer, nacod, wracu, bacan, bucca, draca, ficol, hnecca, sprecan, sticca, macian, liccian, lōcian, prician, drincan, þancian; bucc, blæc, ġeoc (final c), āc, bæc, bōc, brocc, flocc, folc, mioluc, sēoc, weorc, þanc.

**§311, palatal c:** ċēapian, ċēosan, ċinn; ċeaf, ċeafor, ċealc, ċeald, ċealf, ċeorfan, ċeorl, ċēowan, ċīdan, ċīese, ċierran, ċild, ċiriċe; bēċ, lǣċe, smiēċ, weċċan, benċ, birċe, cryċċ, fliċċe, tæċe, myċel, sēċan, streċċan, styċċe, tǣċan, wiċċe, þenċan, drenċan, þynċan, stenċ. **Note 2:** iċ, hwelċ, līċ, piċ, swelċ. Optional e in the source's spellings, such as sēc(e)an, is represented by the corresponding palatal form here.

Do not label ordinary back-vowel or consonant environments in that full velar inventory as “exceptions”: corn, cū, cuman, clǣne, and cwēn belong there as controls. The targeted exclusion tables above isolate the cases a broad front-vowel spelling rule would misclassify. ^[inferred]

## How to use this as a marking reference

Store the **form, position of c, dialect/date, sound category, source, and confidence** together. A bare lemma blacklist would wrongly classify the second c of cyċene and could erase alternations within a paradigm. Preserve a third state, **unresolved**, instead of forcing every unmarked manuscript c into /k/ or /tʃ/. This is a proposed application of the evidence, not a published lexicon. ^[inferred]

## Contradictions and open questions

- Hogg's restrictive account of postvocalic stops and Laker's compressed “front-vowel” summary differ in granularity. Use explicit lexical examples and environments; do not flatten them into one letter rule.
- The time of affrication remains disputed. Wright's conservative phonetic interpretation is historical scholarship, not the latest consensus. Lass and [[entities/margaret-laing|Laing]] show that iċ itself needs multiple historical pathways.
- Modern velars do not settle OE pronunciation. The relative contributions of analogy, native northern developments, and Norse contact are word-specific.
- Kijak's 2022-volume paper (published online in 2023) is represented by the existing vault source note; its journal PDF could not be reopened in this run. Campbell 1959 and Hogg 1979 were not directly read. Hogg's detailed rules here use the earlier vault extract, not a newly obtained full text.
- A truly exhaustive list requires a defined dictionary/corpus, period, dialect, treatment of compounds and variants, and lexical annotation of every c occurrence. That work was not completed by this literature survey.
